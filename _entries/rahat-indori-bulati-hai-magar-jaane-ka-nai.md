There is an unusual confidence in Rahat Indori's refusal. The world calls, but the speaker is not immediately persuaded to follow. He knows that something lies beyond, yet he will not go simply because it beckons. I like this distinction. Throughout life, we encounter invitations, expectations and directions that other people consider right for us. Some are worth following; others may take us away from the things we genuinely wish to pursue. There is a quiet assertion of personal choice in these opening lines: the decision to move must belong to us.

Then comes the extraordinary image of plucking stars and carrying them home. It is almost impossible, wonderfully audacious and full of life. The speaker does not want to return empty-handed from his journey. I do not read this merely as a desire to collect achievements or prove something to the world. To me, it carries the spirit of someone who wants to experience everything the journey has to offer: discoveries, possibilities, friendships, knowledge, beautiful moments and perhaps a few dreams that once seemed too distant to reach.

I find myself smiling at the sheer determination of this image. Life rarely offers a clear route, and there are times when even knowing what we want does not make the path easier. We encounter closed doors, unexpected turns and moments when continuing requires more patience than enthusiasm. Yet there is something important about retaining that sense of possibility. I would not want difficulty to make me cautious about every dream, or experience to persuade me that it is wiser to expect less from life. There must remain some room for the unreasonable ambition that makes us look towards the stars in the first place.

What I appreciate most is the combination of resolve and imagination in these lines. Rahat does not ask for permission to want more from life, nor does he apologise for refusing an empty return. But I hope we never become so occupied with what we wish to achieve that we forget to enjoy the journey or the people who accompany us. There is more than one way of coming home with something valuable. Sometimes it is knowledge, sometimes a memory, sometimes the affection of another person, and sometimes simply the satisfaction of having followed a path that was truly our own. I would like to keep that spirit alive: to remain ambitious without becoming hard, determined without losing tenderness, and curious enough to keep looking towards the stars.
