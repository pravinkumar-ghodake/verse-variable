I find something quietly profound in Iqbal's choice of a lamp to describe reason. A lamp is valuable because it allows a traveller to see the road ahead, to recognise obstacles and to continue without walking entirely in darkness. Yet its light has a boundary. It illuminates the path before it, not everything that exists beyond its reach. Iqbal does not dismiss reason; he gives it its rightful place. It helps us move through the world, but perhaps we sometimes expect it to illuminate much more than it can.

The final two lines draw the image towards the human interior. Behind the walls of a house, countless disturbances may be unfolding, invisible to the lamp outside. I am reminded of how much can remain hidden within a person. Someone may appear composed while carrying grief, uncertainty, unspoken affection or memories that have never quite settled. We see gestures, hear words and form impressions, but the inner life of another person is rarely as visible as we imagine. There are rooms within people that even those closest to them may take years to understand.

I have always been drawn to questions and to the search for explanations. Perhaps that is why this verse makes me pause. The habit of examining things can bring clarity, but it can also make us impatient with what cannot be understood immediately. Not every silence conceals a simple answer, and not every human feeling can be fully explained by identifying its cause. Sometimes understanding begins not with another question, but with the willingness to listen without assuming that we already know.

I return to Iqbal's lamp with a slightly different feeling. Its limitation is not a failure; it is a reminder that seeing something clearly does not mean seeing everything. I would like to retain that humility in the way I read, research and encounter other people. To keep questioning without believing that every question has been exhausted, and to keep seeking knowledge without losing tenderness towards what remains beyond my understanding. Perhaps a thoughtful life requires both: a lamp to guide the way, and the awareness that every house has an inner world of its own.
