I have always liked the idea that the sky we see is not necessarily the limit of what exists. There is something extraordinary in Iqbal's choice of the falcon, a bird whose identity is inseparable from flight. It does not exist merely to remain where it has landed. Its nature is to rise, to move through open skies and to discover what lies beyond the familiar. I read these lines not simply as an encouragement to achieve more, but as a reminder not to mistake our present circumstances for the full extent of our possibilities.

The next couplet makes the thought more personal. Iqbal asks us not to remain entangled in the same cycle of day and night. There is a particular kind of exhaustion in allowing the demands, disappointments and repetitions of everyday life to occupy our entire horizon. Responsibilities matter, and the ordinary rhythms of living cannot simply be abandoned. Yet somewhere within them, we can lose sight of the questions, interests and possibilities that once made us feel alive. The verse reminds me that there is more to life than becoming accustomed to its routines.

I have always felt drawn to questions that open into other questions. Learning, exploring and pursuing an idea rarely follow a single predetermined route. One interest can lead into another, a book can open an unfamiliar field, and an apparently distant subject can become central to the way we understand the world. Not every worthwhile path is clearly laid out in advance, and not every meaningful undertaking comes with an established map or an assurance of recognition. There is a particular freedom in continuing to explore simply because the question matters.

What I take away from these lines is not an instruction to spend life chasing achievements, but an invitation to remain open to its possibilities. The falcon must fly, but that does not mean it has to forget the world beneath its wings. We still need affection, companionship, ordinary happiness and moments in which we can simply enjoy being alive. I would like to retain both sides of this spirit: the determination to keep looking beyond familiar horizons and the sensitivity to appreciate what already makes the journey worthwhile. Perhaps the greatest loss is not failing to reach one particular sky, but convincing ourselves that there are no others left to discover.
