## 1. Purpose

This policy establishes the disclosure principles governing content published by Verse & Variable.

The publication explores literature, poetry, language, philosophy, cultural traditions, and scientific ideas through original commentary and interdisciplinary reflection.

The preparation of an article may involve multiple intellectual and technical contributions, including:

- Original literary authorship.
- Translation and transliteration.
- Historical and linguistic research.
- Scientific research and interpretation.
- Editorial assistance.
- Digital research tools.
- Artificial intelligence-assisted processes.
- External illustrations and resources.
- Financial or institutional support, where applicable.

Readers should be able to distinguish these contributions and understand the circumstances relevant to the material they are reading.

This policy establishes how such information should be disclosed.

## 2. Fundamental Principles

### 2.1 Transparency

Material contributions, relevant interests, and significant assistance should be disclosed accurately.

The purpose of disclosure is to help readers understand the origin and context of published content.

### 2.2 Accurate Authorship

The person responsible for creating an article's original commentary should be distinguished from the creators of the literary works discussed in it.

A poet, lyricist, composer, singer, translator, researcher, and article author perform different intellectual or creative roles.

These roles must not be combined without justification.

### 2.3 Intellectual Accountability

Disclosure of assistance does not transfer editorial responsibility to another person, organisation, software system, or AI tool.

The publication remains responsible for the accuracy, attribution, and presentation of material it publishes.

### 2.4 Relevant Disclosure

Disclosures should be proportionate to their significance.

A material contribution that affects the origin or interpretation of published content deserves greater transparency than an incidental technical adjustment.

### 2.5 No Implied Endorsement

Mentioning a person, organisation, institution, platform, research community, or external service does not imply that it endorses, sponsors, reviews, or formally supports Verse & Variable.

### 2.6 Correction of Disclosures

Where a disclosure is incomplete or incorrect, it should be corrected when reliable information becomes available.

## 3. Scope of Disclosure

This policy applies to public articles, weekly collections, original translations, research discussions, editorial documentation, and other published resources.

Disclosure may be necessary in relation to:

- Article authorship.
- Original literary creators.
- Translation and adaptation.
- AI-assisted research and writing.
- Editorial and technical assistance.
- Research references.
- External images and illustrations.
- Software and digital tools.
- Financial support.
- Institutional affiliations.
- Sponsorship and partnerships.
- Conflicts of interest.
- Permissions and licensing.
- Corrections to previously published disclosures.

Not every article requires every disclosure category.

Only information relevant to the particular material should be included.

## 4. Authorship and Intellectual Contributions

### 4.1 Article Authorship

The author of an individual Verse & Variable article should be the person or persons responsible for its original published commentary and intellectual contribution.

Article authorship should not automatically be assigned to the poet, songwriter, performer, or other creator whose work is being discussed.

### 4.2 Original Literary Creators

The original creators of literary material should receive appropriate attribution.

Depending on the work, this may include:

- Poet.
- Writer.
- Lyricist.
- Composer.
- Playwright.
- Storyteller.
- Other original creator.

The creator's name should be supported by reliable information wherever possible.

### 4.3 Translators and Adapters

Translation and adaptation involve independent linguistic and creative contributions.

Where an existing published translation is used, identify the translator and source.

Where a translation is prepared specifically for Verse & Variable, identify it accurately as an original translation prepared for the publication.

Where a translation involves substantial adaptation, make that distinction clear.

### 4.4 Performers and Musical Contributors

For musical works, distinguish between:

- Lyricist.
- Composer.
- Singer.
- Musician.
- Arranger.
- Producer.
- Performer.
- Translator or adapter.

A performer should not automatically be identified as the lyricist or composer.

### 4.5 Editorial and Technical Assistance

Significant contributions from editors, reviewers, researchers, translators, developers, or other individuals may be acknowledged where appropriate.

An acknowledgement should accurately describe the nature of the contribution.

Do not imply that someone reviewed, approved, or endorsed an article merely because they provided incidental assistance.

## 5. Original Commentary and Third-Party Material

Readers should be able to distinguish the publication's original writing from material obtained or adapted from another source.

### 5.1 Original Commentary

The principal four-paragraph commentary in an individual literary exploration should represent original interpretative writing.

Its intellectual direction and conclusions should not be misrepresented as the work of another author or an automated system.

### 5.2 Direct Quotations

Direct quotations should be identifiable through appropriate formatting and attribution.

Their original source should be cited.

Quotations should not be silently rewritten while retaining the appearance of exact reproduction.

### 5.3 Paraphrased Material

When another person's ideas are paraphrased, the relevant source should be acknowledged where appropriate.

Changing the wording of a source does not eliminate the need for attribution when its intellectual contribution is being used.

### 5.4 Research-Based Claims

Important historical, cultural, linguistic, philosophical, and scientific claims should be supported by appropriate references.

The presence of a bibliography does not automatically establish that every claim in an article is supported by those sources.

### 5.5 External Content

Third-party photographs, illustrations, research figures, recordings, and other resources should be identified according to their actual origin and applicable licence or permission.

Refer to `ASSET_CREDITS.md` and `COPYRIGHT.md`.

## 6. Artificial Intelligence and Automated Assistance

Artificial intelligence and automated tools may be used as part of research, language preparation, editorial development, coding, or other technical activities.

Their use should be disclosed when it makes a material contribution to published content or when disclosure is otherwise relevant.

The use of a tool does not establish the validity, originality, or accuracy of its output.

### 6.1 Possible Uses

AI-assisted processes may include:

- Preliminary literature discovery.
- Research organisation.
- Conceptual exploration.
- Translation assistance.
- Transliteration assistance.
- Grammar and language editing.
- Draft development.
- Scientific terminology exploration.
- Mathematical or computational assistance.
- Code preparation.
- Image generation.
- Visualisation assistance.
- Structural editing.

These activities are not equivalent and may require different disclosure practices.

### 6.2 Material Assistance

Disclosure should be considered when AI or another automated system makes a substantive contribution to:

- The wording of published commentary.
- The preparation of a translation.
- The interpretation of a literary expression.
- The development of a scientific explanation.
- The generation of an illustration.
- The derivation or presentation of a technical result.
- The creation of substantial published code or other content.

The disclosure should describe the actual role of the tool rather than using a generic statement that conceals what was done.

### 6.3 Incidental Assistance

Routine activities such as basic spelling checks, formatting assistance, or ordinary document organisation may not require a separate public disclosure in every article.

However, if an apparently minor activity materially changes the substance, interpretation, or representation of published content, its significance should be reassessed.

### 6.4 No AI Authorship Substitution

An AI system should not be presented as the original human author of a literary work or as an independently accountable scholarly contributor.

Human editorial responsibility remains with the people or publication responsible for the final material.

### 6.5 Verification Responsibility

Material generated or suggested by AI must be independently checked where it concerns factual or substantive claims.

This includes:

- Literary quotations.
- Author attribution.
- Historical information.
- Linguistic explanations.
- Translation.
- Scientific definitions.
- Mathematical expressions.
- Research references.
- Technical instructions.

An AI-generated citation must not be treated as verified until the actual source has been identified and checked.

### 6.6 AI Disclosure Format

Where material AI assistance has been used, an appropriate disclosure may identify:

- The type of assistance.
- The affected part of the article.
- Whether the output was independently checked.
- Whether the material was substantially revised before publication.

Example:

> **AI assistance:** An AI-based language tool assisted with preliminary language refinement. The final wording and editorial interpretation were reviewed by the article's author.

This example should be used only when it accurately describes the actual process.

### 6.7 Machine Translation

Where machine translation contributes materially to a published translation, disclose that contribution when appropriate.

Do not describe an automatically generated translation as independently prepared by a human translator.

### 6.8 AI-Generated Images and Illustrations

Where AI systems generate significant published visual material, identify the nature of the generation process when relevant.

The disclosure should distinguish generated illustrations from photographs, historical images, experimental figures, and original scientific measurements.

An AI-generated scientific illustration must not be presented as an experimental result or a photograph of an actual physical system.

### 6.9 AI and Originality

AI assistance does not establish ownership of third-party intellectual contributions.

Generated content should not be used to conceal unattributed copying, misrepresent authorship, or bypass copyright and licensing requirements.

### 6.10 Detailed AI Practices

The detailed rules governing AI-assisted research, writing, translation, coding, and visual content may be maintained in a separate document:

`AI_ASSISTANCE_POLICY.md`

## 7. Research Sources and Evidence Disclosure

Readers should be able to identify the evidence supporting important factual and technical claims.

### 7.1 Source Identification

Relevant sources should be identified through appropriate citations or references.

Depending on the subject, this may include:

- Original literary publications.
- Historical documents.
- Dictionaries and linguistic references.
- Scholarly books.
- Research papers.
- Experimental studies.
- Technical standards.
- Official software documentation.
- Archival records.

### 7.2 Research Status

Where relevant, distinguish between different research publication types:

- Peer-reviewed research.
- Preprints.
- Conference proceedings.
- Technical reports.
- Theses and dissertations.
- Books and academic monographs.
- Informal research material.

Do not describe an unpublished or unreviewed manuscript as a peer-reviewed study.

### 7.3 Scientific Evidence

Scientific discussion should distinguish between:

- Established principles.
- Mathematical results.
- Theoretical predictions.
- Numerical observations.
- Experimental findings.
- Proposed mechanisms.
- Hypotheses.
- Speculative connections.
- Literary analogies.

The status of a claim should not be strengthened merely through confident language.

### 7.4 Limitations

Where a significant conclusion depends on particular assumptions, datasets, experimental conditions, or modelling choices, disclose those limitations in the article.

A citation to a research paper does not remove the need to explain the limits of the result being discussed.

### 7.5 Research Records

The underlying research process may be documented using:

`SOURCE_REGISTER.md`

The public article should include the relevant references needed for readers to understand and verify its principal claims.

## 8. Financial Support and Funding

Financial support may influence the circumstances in which a publication or particular article is prepared.

Where funding materially supports the creation of published content, the relevant support should be disclosed.

### 8.1 Relevant Funding

Potential disclosures include:

- Research grants.
- Publication grants.
- Institutional funding.
- Project sponsorship.
- Commissioned research.
- Paid editorial assistance.
- Financial support for illustrations or other assets.

### 8.2 Funding Information

Where applicable, identify:

- Funding organisation.
- Relevant grant or project identifier.
- Supported activity.
- Whether the funder influenced the research or editorial process.

### 8.3 No Funding Claims Without Verification

Do not state that an article was independently funded, institutionally funded, or supported by a particular grant without reliable information.

Where no relevant support was received and a declaration is useful, a suitable statement may be:

> **Funding:** No dedicated external funding was received for the preparation of this article.

Use this statement only when it accurately describes the circumstances.

### 8.4 Research Independence

Where a funder, sponsor, or commissioning party influenced the selection, preparation, interpretation, or publication of an article, disclose the relevant involvement.

Do not imply editorial independence from a funding relationship where that independence cannot be established.

## 9. Institutional Affiliations and Relationships

The publication may discuss universities, laboratories, research institutes, academic conferences, journals, publishers, and other organisations.

Mentioning an institution does not create a formal affiliation.

### 9.1 Personal Affiliations

An author's institutional affiliation should be included only where it is accurate and relevant.

Do not use historical associations or previous participation to imply a current appointment.

### 9.2 Publication Independence

Verse & Variable should describe itself as an independent publication unless a formal relationship is established and documented.

### 9.3 Institutional Endorsement

Do not imply that an institution:

- Sponsors the publication.
- Approves the editorial direction.
- Reviews every article.
- Validates the scientific content.
- Endorses an author's interpretation.

Such claims require actual evidence.

### 9.4 Event and Conference Participation

Participation in a conference, presentation at an academic meeting, or attendance at an event does not automatically imply continuing institutional affiliation or formal endorsement.

Describe such participation accurately and in its appropriate context.

## 10. Sponsorship, Partnerships, and Commercial Relationships

Where a publication or article involves commercial support, sponsorship, or a formal partnership, disclose the nature of the relationship.

Relevant circumstances may include:

- Sponsored articles.
- Commissioned contributions.
- Paid product or service references.
- Sponsored illustrations.
- Commercially supported research.
- Formal content partnerships.
- Affiliate arrangements, if introduced.

The disclosure should identify the relationship sufficiently for readers to understand its relevance.

### 10.1 Editorial Independence

Where commercial support exists, identify whether the sponsor had any role in:

- Selecting the subject.
- Preparing the article.
- Reviewing the content.
- Approving the final wording.
- Selecting references.
- Determining publication.

### 10.2 No Implied Partnership

An external link, citation, or acknowledgement should not be presented as evidence of sponsorship or collaboration.

### 10.3 Future Commercial Arrangements

If commercial relationships are introduced in the future, this policy should be updated to describe the relevant disclosure practices.

## 11. Conflicts of Interest

A conflict of interest may arise when a personal, professional, financial, institutional, or other relationship could reasonably influence, or appear to influence, the presentation of material.

Potential conflicts should be identified and evaluated.

### 11.1 Financial Interests

Examples include:

- Financial relationships with a company discussed in an article.
- Investments relevant to a subject being examined.
- Paid consulting.
- Sponsorship.
- Other financial relationships that may materially affect interpretation.

### 11.2 Professional and Institutional Interests

Examples include:

- Employment by an organisation discussed in an article.
- A formal advisory role.
- Institutional responsibilities relevant to a research claim.
- Direct participation in the development of a product or technology being evaluated.

### 11.3 Personal and Intellectual Interests

Examples include:

- Close personal relationships relevant to the subject.
- Direct involvement in a dispute discussed in the article.
- A financial or professional interest in a particular interpretation.
- A strong personal position that materially affects the presentation of contested evidence.

Holding a scholarly position or having a strong intellectual interest does not automatically invalidate an article.

The relevant question is whether a relationship could materially affect the interpretation or presentation and whether readers should be informed.

### 11.4 Managing Conflicts

Depending on the circumstances, appropriate measures may include:

- Disclosure.
- Additional source verification.
- Independent editorial review.
- Reconsideration of the article's scope.
- Correction of misleading language.
- Declining to publish material where independence cannot be maintained.

### 11.5 No Artificial Declarations

Do not publish a blanket statement that an article has no conflicts of interest unless that statement has been considered and is accurate.

## 12. Third-Party Software, Services, and External Resources

The publication may rely on external services and open-source tools.

These resources may contribute to hosting, website design, typography, publishing, content presentation, or research.

Relevant services may include:

- GitHub Pages.
- Jekyll.
- Google Fonts.
- Open-source software libraries.
- Academic databases.
- Digital archives.
- External media platforms.

### 12.1 Technical Acknowledgement

Where appropriate, identify the tools and services that materially contribute to the publication's operation.

### 12.2 Software Licensing

Use the applicable licence of each external software dependency.

Do not imply that the repository's MIT License automatically applies to independently licensed third-party software or assets.

### 12.3 External Research Resources

A reference to an external publication or platform does not imply endorsement by its owner or publisher.

### 12.4 External Embeds

Where external media or services are embedded, consider their privacy, security, accessibility, and licensing implications.

Refer to `ASSET_CREDITS.md` and `PRIVACY.md`.

## 13. Editorial Independence

The editorial direction of Verse & Variable should remain distinguishable from external influences.

The selection, interpretation, and presentation of literary material should be governed by the publication's established editorial principles.

### 13.1 Independent Interpretation

An article's interpretation should be identified as the publication's own considered reading unless it is quoting or explicitly adopting another person's interpretation.

### 13.2 No Implied Review or Approval

The publication should not claim that an article has received formal peer review, institutional approval, or expert certification unless that process actually occurred.

### 13.3 External Opinions

An individual cited, acknowledged, or mentioned in an article should not automatically be treated as endorsing the article's conclusions.

### 13.4 Disagreement

Editorial independence includes the ability to maintain a reasoned interpretative position when alternative views exist.

However, factual and scientific claims remain subject to appropriate evidence and correction.

## 14. Disclosure Placement

Disclosure should appear where it is reasonably useful to readers.

Depending on its nature, it may be included:

- Within the article's attribution section.
- In a translator's note.
- In a research-method note.
- In a funding statement.
- In an acknowledgement.
- In a dedicated disclosure section.
- Within relevant publication documentation.

A material disclosure should not be hidden in an unrelated repository file when it directly affects the interpretation of an article.

### 14.1 Article-Level Disclosure

Where disclosure is relevant to a particular article, provide it on that article's page.

### 14.2 Publication-Level Disclosure

Information about general publication practices may be maintained in:

- `PRIVACY.md`
- `ASSET_CREDITS.md`
- `ACKNOWLEDGEMENTS.md`
- `AI_ASSISTANCE_POLICY.md`
- Other applicable policies.

### 14.3 Avoid Repetitive Disclosures

Do not repeat lengthy general disclosures in every article when a concise explanation and an accessible policy reference are sufficient.

However, do not use general documentation as a substitute for disclosing a material article-specific relationship.

## 15. Recommended Disclosure Format

Where an article requires a dedicated disclosure section, the following structure may be used.

### Disclosure

**Article authorship:** [Actual article author.]

**Original literary work:** [Original creator and verified source.]

**Translation:** [Original translation / Published translation / Adaptation.]

**AI assistance:** [Relevant use, where material.]

**Research assistance:** [Significant assistance, where applicable.]

**Funding:** [Relevant support, where applicable.]

**Institutional affiliation:** [Verified relationship, where relevant.]

**Conflicts of interest:** [Relevant disclosure, where applicable.]

**Third-party materials:** [Sources, permissions, and licences, where required.]

Remove fields that are not relevant rather than publishing unnecessary empty declarations.

Do not use this template to imply that every category has been independently certified or audited.

## 16. Example Disclosure Statements

The following examples illustrate appropriate wording. They should be used only when they accurately describe the actual circumstances.

### 16.1 Original Translation

> **Translation:** The English translation was prepared specifically for Verse & Variable from the original literary text.

### 16.2 Published Translation

> **Translation:** The English rendering is by [Translator] and is identified with its original publication source.

### 16.3 AI-Assisted Language Editing

> **AI assistance:** An AI-based language tool assisted with preliminary language refinement. The final article was reviewed and approved by its human author.

### 16.4 AI-Assisted Research

> **Research assistance:** AI tools were used to help identify preliminary research directions. The factual claims and references included in the published article were independently checked against identifiable sources.

### 16.5 AI-Generated Illustration

> **Visual disclosure:** This illustration was generated using an AI-assisted image-generation process. It is a conceptual representation and does not depict an experimentally observed physical system.

### 16.6 External Funding

> **Funding:** This article received support from [Organisation / Programme] for [specific activity].

### 16.7 No Relevant External Funding

> **Funding:** No dedicated external funding was received for the preparation of this article.

### 16.8 Conflicting Interest

> **Conflict of interest:** The author has a relevant professional relationship with [Organisation]. This relationship is disclosed because the article discusses a subject connected with that organisation.

### 16.9 Editorial Assistance

> **Acknowledgement:** The author thanks [Name] for assistance with [specific contribution].

A person's acknowledgement should reflect their actual contribution and, where appropriate, their preference for public recognition.

## 17. Disclosure and Corrections

Disclosures may become inaccurate when circumstances change or when previously unknown information becomes available.

Examples include:

- Discovery of an omitted funding relationship.
- Correction of a translation credit.
- Identification of an AI-generated image.
- Discovery of an incorrect institutional affiliation.
- Identification of an undisclosed external contribution.
- Correction of an inaccurate licensing statement.

Where a disclosure is materially inaccurate:

1. Re-examine the relevant information.
2. Establish the correct facts.
3. Update the affected article or publication document.
4. Add a correction note when appropriate.
5. Preserve the relevant publication history.
6. Update associated metadata where necessary.

Refer to `CORRECTIONS_POLICY.md`.

## 18. Privacy and Confidentiality

Disclosure must not become a reason to expose private or confidential information.

The publication should disclose relevant relationships and contributions without unnecessarily publishing:

- Private contact information.
- Confidential correspondence.
- Sensitive personal identifiers.
- Restricted research documents.
- Unnecessary personal details.
- Information about individuals who have not agreed to public acknowledgement where such acknowledgement is not otherwise justified.

Where a disclosure creates a privacy concern, consider a more limited but still meaningful description.

Refer to `PRIVACY.md`.

Before publishing an article, consider the following.

- [ ] Lyricist, composer, and performer roles are correct.
- [ ] Significant contributors are appropriately acknowledged.

### Research and AI assistance

- [ ] Research sources are identifiable.
- [ ] AI assistance has been disclosed where materially relevant.
- [ ] Generated factual claims have been checked.
- [ ] Machine-assisted translations are appropriately reviewed.
- [ ] AI-generated visual material is identified where relevant.

### Funding and relationships

- [ ] Relevant financial support is disclosed.
- [ ] Institutional affiliations are accurate.
- [ ] Sponsorship and partnerships are correctly described.
- [ ] Relevant conflicts of interest are considered.
- [ ] External endorsement is not implied without evidence.

### Third-party material

- [ ] Images and illustrations have appropriate credits.
- [ ] Software dependencies are documented.
- [ ] Translation rights are considered.
- [ ] Required permissions and licences are addressed.

### Final presentation

- [ ] Disclosures are clear and proportionate.
- [ ] Article-specific information appears on the relevant page.

## 21. Continuing Commitment

The value of an intellectual publication depends not only on the information it presents but also on the clarity with which it communicates the origins and circumstances of that information.

Literary works deserve accurate attribution. Translations deserve recognition of their translators. Scientific claims deserve appropriate references. Significant assistance deserves transparent disclosure. Financial and institutional relationships deserve accurate representation.

Verse & Variable seeks to maintain an environment in which readers can distinguish original contribution from external assistance, interpretation from evidence, and editorial independence from institutional endorsement.

Transparency should strengthen intellectual trust without becoming an exercise in unnecessary self-promotion or artificial declarations.
