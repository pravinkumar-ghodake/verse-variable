*From the word to the wave.*

## Why Verse & Variable?

I have always been drawn to things that invite careful attention. A few lines of poetry can remain with me long after I have finished reading them. Sometimes it is the beauty of the language; sometimes it is an observation about human nature, an unexpected thought or an emotion expressed with an economy of words that seems almost impossible to improve upon. My curiosity also extends to scientific questions and the study of the physical world. My literary interests are not confined to any one national tradition; I am equally drawn to voices, ideas and expressions from India and around the world. These interests are part of my intellectual life, but they do not need to converge in every reading. Verse & Variable grew from a lasting affection for literature and a desire to explore its many voices, ideas and experiences with care.

The journal begins with literature, particularly the extraordinary range of poetry and lyrical expression found across India. Its present editorial focus is Indian literature, explored through its many languages, histories and literary sensibilities. Urdu shayari, Hindi poetry, Marathi kavita, Kannada vachanas, Tamil literature, Malayalam poetry and Telugu writing each possess their own histories, expressions and literary worlds. I want to explore this diversity without reducing it to a collection of popular quotations. A relatively unfamiliar couplet can be as meaningful as a celebrated ghazal; an overlooked passage in a book can carry more intellectual weight than a widely circulated line. The selection is guided by literary quality, emotional resonance, philosophical depth and the possibility of a worthwhile reflection.

The original expression is always important. Its language, script, rhythm, imagery and cultural context deserve to be preserved rather than treated as material that simply needs conversion into English. English is the language through which the journal communicates its interpretations, but it does not replace the original literary voice. Whether the source is Urdu, Marathi, Hindi, Kannada, Tamil, Malayalam or Telugu, the intention is to allow readers to encounter the expression in its original form before exploring its English translation and the thoughts it inspires. The journal's present scope is Indian literature; this is the focus of the archive, not a boundary around my own reading or wider literary interests.

## The Perspective I Bring

What makes this journal particularly personal is the way I approach the material. I do not read literature only as a reader, nor do I approach every expression as an academic exercise. I bring different parts of my intellectual and human experience into the reading: the curiosity of a student, the investigative instincts of a researcher, the analytical perspective of a scientist, and the sensibilities of someone who finds meaning in books, music, cinema, sport, philosophy and everyday human experiences. My reading extends across Indian and international literary traditions, as well as other fields of thought. Some expressions invite an intimate reflection; others raise questions about knowledge, time, memory, identity, society or the nature of existence. I would rather follow the direction in which a particular expression leads than impose a predetermined interpretation upon it.

My research background is rooted in theoretical, computational and experimental mechanics, including nonlinear vibrations, nonlinear elastic waves, nonlinear ultrasonics, harmonic generation and scattering, rate-independent hysteresis, wave mixing, mechanical metamaterials and inverse design. This work has shaped how I observe systems, recognise patterns and approach unfamiliar questions. It is an important part of my intellectual background and the perspective I bring to the world, but it is not a framework that every literary expression must enter.

Scientific knowledge may enter a reflection when it genuinely helps illuminate an idea or question raised by the literary work. When that happens, the connection should have intellectual substance and respect the foundations of both fields. But I do not approach a poem looking for a scientific parallel, nor do I treat a literary image as though it were a statement about a physical mechanism.

Other reflections may remain entirely within the world of language, emotion, philosophy, memory, relationships, society or personal experience. The literary expression itself determines the direction. It does not need to establish a connection with science to deserve attention.

## The Human Dimension

Not everything needs a scientific interpretation. I am equally interested in expressions that speak directly about love, friendship, longing, solitude, vulnerability, ambition, self-respect, failure, contentment and the contradictions that accompany human life. A beautiful sher does not become more valuable because it can be connected to an equation. Sometimes its greatest achievement is that it captures an experience that ordinary language struggles to express. In such instances, the reflection should remain close to the human experience and allow the poetry to retain its emotional character.

My wider reading and interests also influence the way I encounter literature. I read literary works from India and across the world, alongside books on science, philosophy, psychology, human behaviour, learning and personal development. Cinema, music and sport have introduced me to different expressions of creativity, discipline, storytelling and human achievement. I am particularly interested in the thinking behind exceptional work: the patience required to master a craft, the uncertainty involved in pursuing an idea, the complexity of relationships, and the quiet decisions that shape an individual's journey. These are not separate interests competing for space; they are different ways through which I encounter the world.

## How the Journal Is Written

Verse & Variable publishes three literary explorations each week. Every selection receives its own dedicated article page, while a separate weekly page brings the three entries together. The original expression appears in its original script, followed by an English translation, relevant attribution and source information. Each reflection is written in no more than four concise prose paragraphs, in a continuous first-person style. The writing is deliberately calm, natural and considered. I prefer an idea that unfolds gradually to one that announces its importance too loudly.

The reflections are not intended to be motivational messages, academic abstracts or technical explanations disguised as literary commentary. They are personal essays in which a literary expression becomes the starting point for a deeper conversation. A reflection may follow an author's imagery into a personal observation, explore a philosophical question, consider memory or relationships, or examine a wider aspect of human experience. On occasion, scientific knowledge may offer a meaningful further perspective. When it does not, there is no reason to introduce it. The direction will vary because the literary expressions themselves vary.

Attribution and intellectual integrity are equally important. Literary expressions must be traced to their identifiable authors and original works wherever possible. A performance is not automatically the authorship of its lyrics, and a popular online attribution is not sufficient evidence of origin. Where an attribution remains uncertain, that uncertainty should be disclosed. Translations must distinguish between literal meaning and literary interpretation, and existing translations must be credited appropriately. The journal will also respect the copyright of poets, authors, lyricists, composers, translators and other rights holders, using appropriately limited excerpts or material for which permission or another valid legal basis exists.

## The Author

**Pravinkumar Ghodake** (full name as it appears on records: **Pravinkumar Ramchandra Ghodake**) is an independent researcher whose intellectual interests span theoretical, computational and experimental mechanics, nonlinear ultrasonics, nonlinear elastic waves, mechanical metamaterials and inverse design. His wider interests extend to literature from India and around the world, poetry, shayari, books, philosophy, music, cinema, sport and scientific inquiry.

Verse & Variable is not an extension of a research paper, nor is it intended to function as a conventional professional portfolio. It is a personal space where my different interests can coexist without requiring one to explain or validate the other. My scientific background is part of the perspective I bring, while literature remains free to be encountered on its own terms. Analytical thinking and the simple pleasure of reading a beautiful line need not compete.
