# Lafz se Lehar tak

*From the word to the wave.*

## Why Verse & Variable?

I have always been drawn to things that invite more than one way of understanding them. A few lines of poetry can remain with me long after I have finished reading them. Sometimes it is the beauty of the language; sometimes it is an idea, an observation about human nature, or an emotion expressed with an economy of words that seems almost impossible to improve upon. I find a similar fascination in scientific questions. A physical phenomenon that initially appears straightforward can reveal extraordinary complexity when examined more closely. Verse & Variable grew from my interest in bringing these two worlds into the same intellectual space.

The journal begins with literature, particularly the extraordinary range of poetry and lyrical expression found across India. Urdu shayari, Hindi poetry, Marathi kavita, Kannada vachanas, Tamil literature, Malayalam poetry and Telugu writing each possess their own histories, expressions and literary sensibilities. I want to explore this diversity without reducing it to a collection of popular quotations. A relatively unfamiliar couplet can be as meaningful as a celebrated ghazal; an overlooked passage in a book can carry more intellectual weight than a widely circulated line. The selection is guided by literary quality, emotional resonance, philosophical depth and the possibility of a worthwhile reflection.

The original expression is always important. Its language, script, rhythm, imagery and cultural context deserve to be preserved rather than treated as material that simply needs conversion into English. English is the language through which the journal communicates its interpretations, but it does not replace the original literary voice. Whether the source is Urdu, Marathi, Hindi, Kannada, Tamil, Malayalam or Telugu, the intention is to allow readers to encounter the expression in its original form before exploring its English translation and the thoughts it inspires. The wider South Asian literary tradition also has a place here, approached through literary and artistic appreciation rather than political boundaries.

## Beyond Literary Interpretation

What makes this journal particularly personal is the way I approach the material. I do not read literature only as a reader, nor do I approach every expression as an academic exercise. I bring different parts of my intellectual and human experience into the reading: the curiosity of a student, the investigative instincts of a researcher, the analytical perspective of a scientist, and the sensibilities of someone who finds meaning in books, music, cinema, sport, philosophy and everyday human experiences. Some expressions invite an intimate reflection; others raise questions about knowledge, time, memory, identity, society or the nature of existence. I would rather follow the direction in which a particular expression leads than impose a predetermined interpretation upon it.

My research interests provide another dimension to these explorations. My engagement with theoretical, computational and experimental mechanics has taken me through nonlinear vibrations, nonlinear elastic waves, nonlinear ultrasonics, harmonic generation and scattering, rate-independent hysteresis, wave mixing, mechanical metamaterials and inverse design. These subjects have gradually shaped the way I observe systems and ask questions about their behaviour. Moving beyond linear approximations, for instance, reveals how amplitude, interactions, material response and the history of excitation can fundamentally alter the behaviour of a physical system. The resulting phenomena can be surprisingly rich, involving higher harmonics, frequency interactions and complex propagation patterns.

I find opportunities to connect these scientific ideas with literary expressions, provided the connection has genuine intellectual substance. A couplet about memory might lead towards the path-dependent response of a hysteretic system. An expression about transformation might invite a discussion of nonlinear dynamics. A poem about coexistence or contradiction might open a reflection on interacting waves, interference or frequency mixing. There are also fascinating relationships between nonlinear elasticity and nonlinear optics, where different physical mechanisms can give rise to mathematically related phenomena such as harmonic generation and nonlinear wave mixing. These connections are not intended to suggest that a poet was describing a scientific theory before its time. They are ways of extending a literary observation into a different field of inquiry while respecting the intellectual foundations of both.

## The Human Dimension

Not everything needs a scientific interpretation. I am equally interested in expressions that speak directly about love, friendship, longing, solitude, vulnerability, ambition, self-respect, failure, contentment and the contradictions that accompany human life. A beautiful sher does not become more valuable because it can be connected to an equation. Sometimes its greatest achievement is that it captures an experience that ordinary language struggles to express. In such instances, the reflection should remain close to the human experience and allow the poetry to retain its emotional character.

My wider reading and interests also influence the way I encounter literature. Books on science, philosophy, psychology, human behaviour, learning and personal development have shaped my curiosity. Cinema, music and sport have introduced me to different expressions of creativity, discipline, storytelling and human achievement. I am particularly interested in the thinking behind exceptional work: the patience required to master a craft, the uncertainty involved in pursuing an idea, the complexity of relationships, and the quiet decisions that shape an individual's journey. These are not separate interests competing for space; they are different ways through which I encounter the world.

## How the Journal Is Written

Verse & Variable publishes three literary explorations each week. Every selection receives its own dedicated article page, while a separate weekly page brings the three entries together. The original expression appears in its original script, followed by an English translation, relevant attribution and source information. Each reflection is written in four substantial prose paragraphs, in a continuous first-person style. The writing is deliberately calm, natural and considered. I prefer an idea that unfolds gradually to one that announces its importance too loudly.

The reflections are not intended to be motivational messages, academic abstracts or technical explanations disguised as literary commentary. They are personal essays in which a literary expression becomes the starting point for a deeper conversation. The writing may move from an author's imagery to a philosophical question, from a personal observation to a scientific mechanism, or from an apparently simple line to a complex idea in mechanics or physics. The direction will vary because the expressions themselves vary. What remains consistent is the effort to think carefully, write honestly and avoid connections that do not withstand examination.

Attribution and intellectual integrity are equally important. Literary expressions must be traced to their identifiable authors and original works wherever possible. A performance is not automatically the authorship of its lyrics, and a popular online attribution is not sufficient evidence of origin. Where an attribution remains uncertain, that uncertainty should be disclosed. Translations must distinguish between literal meaning and literary interpretation, and existing translations must be credited appropriately. The journal will also respect the copyright of poets, authors, lyricists, composers, translators and other rights holders, using appropriately limited excerpts or material for which permission or another valid legal basis exists.

## The Author

**Pravinkumar Ghodake** (full name as it appears on records: **Pravinkumar Ramchandra Ghodake**) is an independent researcher whose intellectual interests span theoretical, computational and experimental mechanics, nonlinear ultrasonics, nonlinear elastic waves, mechanical metamaterials and inverse design. His wider interests extend to Indian literature, poetry, shayari, books, philosophy, music, cinema, sport and interdisciplinary scientific thought.

Verse & Variable is not an extension of a research paper, nor is it intended to function as a conventional professional portfolio. It is a personal space in which these different interests can meet without being confined to a single academic discipline or cultural expression. The journal allows literary curiosity to coexist with scientific investigation, and analytical thinking to coexist with the simple pleasure of reading a beautiful line.
