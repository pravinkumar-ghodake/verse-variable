## 1. Purpose

This document establishes a consistent system for identifying, recording, reviewing, and managing the intellectual property rights and permissions associated with material used by Verse & Variable.

The publication may incorporate or reference material from multiple creators, publishers, archives, research organisations, cultural collections, software projects, and individual contributors.

Each material type may involve different ownership arrangements, copyright rules, licences, access conditions, and restrictions.

This register is intended to help ensure that material is not published or redistributed without an appropriate rights assessment.

Its objectives are to:

- Identify the creators and relevant rights holders.
- Record the provenance of third-party material.
- Document licences and permission arrangements.
- Identify restrictions on reproduction and modification.
- Record attribution requirements.
- Distinguish owned material from licensed material.
- Track public-domain assessments.
- Identify unresolved rights questions.
- Maintain evidence of permission where appropriate.
- Support corrections, takedowns, and future rights reviews.
- Prevent accidental assumptions about ownership or reuse.

This document is an editorial rights-management framework. It is not legal advice and does not establish ownership of any material merely because an entry has been recorded.

## 2. Scope

This register applies to material used, reproduced, adapted, displayed, referenced, hosted, or distributed through the publication.

- Maps.
- Audio recordings.

## 3. Fundamental Rights Principles

### 3.1 Ownership Must Be Established

Do not assume that material belongs to the person who uploaded it, published it online, performed it, or shared it privately.

Identify the relevant creator, rights holder, licensor, or authorised representative wherever reasonably possible.

### 3.2 Attribution Is Not Permission

Naming the original author or source does not automatically authorise reproduction, distribution, translation, modification, or other restricted uses.

### 3.3 Access Is Not Ownership

Material that is freely accessible online is not automatically available for unrestricted reuse.

### 3.4 Permission Must Match the Intended Use

A permission to quote a passage may not authorise:

- Reproducing the entire work.
- Publishing a complete translation.
- Creating an adaptation.
- Hosting an audio recording.
- Including the material in a downloadable collection.
- Redistributing it through another platform.
- Using it in promotional material.

The actual permission terms should be recorded.

### 3.5 Different Rights May Apply to One Item

A single item may contain several independently protected elements.

For example, a recording of a song may involve rights relating to:

- The lyrics.
- The musical composition.
- The sound recording.
- The performer.
- The underlying literary work.
- The visual artwork used with the recording.

The relevant rights should be examined separately.

### 3.6 Ethics and Privacy Remain Relevant

Legal permission does not automatically resolve privacy, confidentiality, informed consent, cultural sensitivity, or research ethics concerns.

### 3.7 No Unsupported Rights Claims

Do not claim ownership, a particular licence, public-domain status, or permission without an identifiable basis.

## 4. Rights Categories

Every material item should be assigned an appropriate rights category.

| Category | Description |
|---|---|
| Original project material | Created by the publication or its authorised contributors |
| Contributor-owned material | Created and owned by an individual contributor |
| Third-party copyrighted material | Controlled by an external rights holder |
| Licensed material | Used under an identified licence |
| Permission-based material | Used with specific authorisation |
| Public-domain material | Determined to be free of applicable copyright restrictions in the relevant jurisdiction |
| Traditional or collective material | Associated with an oral, folk, or community tradition |
| Government or institutional material | Subject to the relevant legal and institutional rights framework |
| Open-source software | Distributed under an identified software licence |
| Open-data material | Distributed under an identified data licence |
| Unknown or disputed status | Ownership or lawful use has not been established |
| AI-generated material | Generated or substantially assisted through an AI system, subject to applicable rights and terms |
| Mixed-rights material | Contains multiple separately controlled elements |

The categories are descriptive. Assigning a category does not itself establish a legal conclusion.

## 5. Register Structure

Each material item should receive a unique internal identifier.

Suggested format:

`VV-RP-YYYY-NNN`

Where:

- `VV` identifies Verse & Variable.
- `RP` identifies the rights and permissions register.
- `YYYY` represents the year of registration.
- `NNN` represents a sequential identifier.

Example:

`VV-RP-2026-001`

Identifiers should remain stable even when an article is updated or the rights status changes.

## 7. Literary Works and Poetry

Literary works require particular care because their creators, publishers, translators, and rights holders may be different people or organisations.

### 7.1 Original Literary Works

For poems, ghazals, nazms, rubaiyat, literary excerpts, and other written works, identify:

- Original author.
- Original language.
- Work title.
- Original publication or collection.
- Edition or version.
- Rights holder, where identifiable.
- Relevant copyright status.
- Intended form and amount of reproduction.

### 7.2 Song Lyrics

Song lyrics should be assessed separately from the corresponding musical composition and sound recording.

Where relevant, identify:

- Lyricist.
- Composer.
- Publisher.
- Relevant rights holder.
- Recording artist, where recordings are involved.
- Licence or permission arrangement.

Do not assume that a song's performer owns the lyrics.

### 7.3 Published Translations

A translation may have independent copyright protection even when the underlying original work is in the public domain.

Identify:

- Original author.
- Translator.
- Translation title or edition.
- Publisher.
- Rights holder.
- Permission or licence conditions.

Do not reproduce a published translation as though it were an original translation prepared for the publication.

### 7.4 Newly Prepared Translations

Record:

- Name of translator.
- Source text.
- Translation date.
- Translation method.
- Rights and publication agreement.
- Any use of AI-assisted translation.

Where the translator is a contributor, identify the agreed attribution and publication terms.

### 7.5 Traditional and Anonymous Works

For traditional or anonymous material:

- Identify the known cultural or linguistic origin.
- Record the source from which the version was obtained.
- Distinguish a traditional expression from a particular published collection or performance.
- Identify separate rights in modern editions, recordings, illustrations, and translations.
- Avoid inventing ownership information.
- Consider community expectations and cultural restrictions.

The absence of an identified individual author does not automatically mean that every associated modern recording, transcription, translation, or edition is unrestricted.

## 8. Research Articles, Books, and Academic Publications

Research publications may contain several independently protected elements.

Examples include:

- Article text.
- Research figures.
- Photographs.
- Graphical abstracts.
- Tables.
- Maps.
- Supplementary data.
- Software.
- Published translations.

### 8.1 Bibliographic Citation

A complete bibliographic citation should be maintained even when the article uses only a short quotation or discusses a research result without reproducing the original material.

### 8.2 Research Figures

Before reproducing a published figure, identify whether:

- It belongs to the author.
- The publisher controls reproduction rights.
- The paper is published under an open licence.
- The relevant figure has a separate credit line.
- Additional permission is necessary.

Do not assume that permission to read a research paper includes permission to reproduce its figures.

### 8.3 Adapted Figures

If a figure is modified, verify that the licence or permission permits the modification.

Identify the original source and describe the adaptation accurately.

Do not present an adapted figure as an entirely original illustration.

### 8.4 Tables and Extracted Data

Distinguish between:

- Copyright in the presentation of a table.
- Rights in the underlying data.
- Database rights, where applicable.
- Licence conditions governing the dataset.
- Confidentiality or contractual restrictions.

### 8.5 Preprints and Conference Material

Record publication status.

An author may have distributed a preprint while a publisher controls particular rights in the final published article or its presentation.

Check the actual terms rather than assuming that the two versions have identical permissions.

## 9. Images, Artwork, and Photographs

Images require an explicit rights assessment.

### 9.1 Source Identification

Record:

- Creator or photographer.
- Original publication.
- Rights holder.
- Image URL or collection reference.
- Licence.
- Date accessed.
- Permission details.

### 9.2 Photographs of People

Consider both copyright and the privacy, consent, and applicable personality or image rights of identifiable individuals.

Permission from the photographer does not necessarily establish permission to publish an identifiable person's image in every context.

### 9.3 Historical Photographs

A photograph may have a separate rights status from the underlying historical subject or event.

Consider:

- Date of creation.
- Creator.
- Ownership.
- Applicable jurisdiction.
- Archive terms.
- Modern digitisation or reproduction conditions.

### 9.4 Illustrations and Artwork

Identify the artist and relevant rights holder.

Check whether modification, cropping, recolouring, or inclusion in other visual designs is permitted.

### 9.5 AI-Generated Images

Where AI-generated imagery is used:

- Identify the tool or production process where relevant.
- Record relevant service terms.
- Check third-party reference-image permissions.
- Avoid representing generated imagery as an authentic photograph or historical record.
- Consider applicable rights and disclosure requirements.
- Record any material human contribution.

Do not assume that AI generation automatically resolves all intellectual property concerns.

## 10. Audio and Video Material

Audio and video may involve several rights holders.

Examples include:

- Sound recordings.
- Musical compositions.
- Lyrics.
- Performances.
- Interviews.
- Documentary footage.
- Educational recordings.
- Broadcast material.
- Public lectures.

### 10.1 Rights Assessment

Identify the relevant rights in:

- The underlying work.
- The performance.
- The recording.
- The production.
- The distribution or hosting arrangement.

### 10.2 Interview Recordings

For interview material, separately consider:

- Recording consent.
- Permission to publish excerpts.
- Permission to publish the full recording.
- Identification preferences.
- Third-party information.
- Retention arrangements.

### 10.3 Embedding External Media

An external video or audio link does not automatically grant permission to download, reproduce, edit, or independently host that media.

Respect platform terms and any relevant licence conditions.

### 10.4 Transcription

A transcription may contain protected expression or personal information.

Its use should be assessed separately from the recording itself.

## 11. Software, Code, and Digital Resources

The publication may use software and other digital resources in its website and research explanations.

### 11.1 Software Register

Identify:

- Software name.
- Version.
- Developer or organisation.
- Source repository.
- Licence.
- Modification status.
- Attribution requirements.
- Dependencies.
- Restrictions.

### 11.2 Open-Source Licences

Do not assume that all open-source licences have identical conditions.

Examples include:

- MIT License.
- Apache License 2.0.
- BSD licences.
- GNU General Public License.
- GNU Lesser General Public License.
- Mozilla Public License.
- Other approved software licences.

Check the actual licence and its requirements.

### 11.3 Website Dependencies

For each important dependency, record its origin and licence where practicable.

Examples include:

- JavaScript libraries.
- CSS frameworks.
- Jekyll plugins.
- Icon libraries.
- Themes.
- Website templates.
- Build tools.

### 11.4 Code Reuse

If code is copied or adapted from another source, comply with its licence and preserve required copyright and attribution notices.

### 11.5 MIT Repository Licence

The repository contains a default MIT licence.

The scope of that licence must be interpreted in relation to the actual material and any express exclusions or separate rights notices.

Do not assume that the repository's MIT notice grants rights in third-party literature, translations, photographs, recordings, research figures, or other content that the project does not own.

Where the project intends different licensing arrangements for code, editorial content, and third-party assets, those distinctions should be stated explicitly and accurately.

## 12. Fonts, Typography, and Design Resources

Fonts and visual design resources may have separate licence conditions.

For each font or design asset, record:

- Font or resource name.
- Designer or foundry.
- Source.
- Version.
- Licence.
- Web-embedding permission.
- Self-hosting permission.
- Modification restrictions.
- Attribution requirements.

### 12.1 Web Fonts

A font available for desktop use is not automatically authorised for web embedding.

Check the licence for the intended distribution and technical use.

### 12.2 Multilingual Fonts

Particular attention should be given to fonts used for:

- Urdu.
- Hindi.
- Marathi.
- Kannada.
- Tamil.
- Malayalam.
- Telugu.

Check that the selected fonts support the relevant scripts and that the licence permits the intended hosting and distribution.

### 12.3 Icon Collections

Record the licence and attribution conditions of icon libraries.

Do not assume that an icon is free of restrictions merely because it is available in a design tool or development package.

## 13. Data and Research Datasets

Datasets may be governed by copyright, database rights, confidentiality restrictions, privacy obligations, contractual terms, or separate access conditions.

### 13.1 Dataset Identification

Record:

- Dataset title.
- Creator.
- Issuing organisation.
- Version.
- Publication date.
- Repository.
- DOI or permanent identifier.
- Licence.
- Access date.

### 13.2 Reuse Conditions

Determine whether the dataset permits:

- Analysis.
- Reproduction.
- Redistribution.
- Modification.
- Commercial use.
- Derivative datasets.
- Publication of selected results.

### 13.3 Sensitive Data

A dataset may be publicly accessible but still contain personal or sensitive information.

Assess privacy, re-identification, confidentiality, and ethical restrictions before use.

### 13.4 Derived Data

When an article includes processed or derived data, document the original dataset and relevant transformation methods.

### 13.5 Proprietary Data

Do not include restricted datasets or confidential research records without the appropriate authorisation.

## 14. Public-Domain Assessment

Material may be in the public domain because copyright protection has expired, never applied, been relinquished, or otherwise does not restrict the particular use under the relevant legal framework.

The assessment should be specific to the material and jurisdiction.

### 14.1 Information to Record

Identify:

- Original creator.
- Date of creation.
- Date of publication, where relevant.
- Date of the creator's death, where relevant.
- Applicable jurisdiction.
- Relevant legal basis.
- Any separate rights in the edition or reproduction.

### 14.2 Separate Rights

Consider whether rights may still exist in:

- Modern translations.
- Critical editions.
- Scholarly introductions.
- Editorial notes.
- Photographic reproductions.
- Digital scans.
- Illustrations.
- Audio recordings.
- Performances.

### 14.3 Uncertain Status

If the public-domain status cannot be established confidently, mark it as uncertain and do not treat the material as unrestricted.

### 14.4 No Blanket Assumptions

Do not assume that:

- An old work is necessarily public domain everywhere.
- An anonymous work is unrestricted.
- A government document can always be freely reproduced.
- A digitally accessible book is available for unrestricted reuse.
- A public-domain original makes every translation public domain.

## 15. Creative Commons and Other Open Licences

Open licences provide permission under defined conditions.

Each licence should be reviewed in its actual version.

### 15.1 Creative Commons

Relevant licence families may include:

- CC BY.
- CC BY-SA.
- CC BY-ND.
- CC BY-NC.
- CC BY-NC-SA.
- CC BY-NC-ND.
- CC0.

Do not treat these licences as interchangeable.

### 15.2 Attribution

Where attribution is required, retain the information necessary to identify the creator, title, source, licence, and changes made where applicable.

### 15.3 Share-Alike

Where a share-alike condition applies, determine whether the proposed adaptation triggers that condition and identify the required licensing approach.

### 15.4 Non-Commercial Conditions

Do not assume that a use complies with a non-commercial restriction merely because the publication is independent or not currently generating revenue.

Assess the actual circumstances.

### 15.5 No-Derivatives Conditions

A no-derivatives licence may restrict publication of translations, adaptations, or other modified versions.

Check the intended use carefully.

### 15.6 CC0

Record the exact material to which the CC0 dedication applies.

Do not assume that a CC0 label for one dataset or image releases unrelated rights in other material associated with it.

### 15.7 Licence Changes

A change in the licence displayed on a source website should not automatically be assumed to retroactively invalidate or modify an earlier valid grant.

Retain appropriate evidence of the licence applicable when the material was obtained and used.

## 16. Contributor-Submitted Material

Contributions should have a clear rights arrangement before publication.

### 16.1 Contributor Ownership

A contributor should identify material they created and the extent of their ownership.

### 16.2 Joint Contributions

Where multiple individuals contributed, determine whether all necessary permissions or agreements have been obtained.

### 16.3 Third-Party Material

Contributors should identify third-party content embedded in their submissions.

### 16.4 Publication Permission

A publication agreement should clearly specify the rights required by Verse & Variable.

Possible permissions include:

- Hosting.
- Displaying.
- Reproducing.
- Formatting.
- Correcting.
- Translating.
- Distributing.
- Archiving.
- Creating accessible versions.

Not every contribution requires every permission.

### 16.5 No Automatic Transfer

Submission alone should not be interpreted as transferring ownership of the contributor's work.

Any transfer, exclusive licence, non-exclusive licence, or other arrangement must be communicated clearly and agreed appropriately.

### 16.6 Public Repository Publication

Do not upload confidential agreements or unpublished material to a public repository before the relevant arrangements are established.

## 17. Permission Request Procedure

When permission is required, the editor or contributor should establish the following.

### 17.1 Identify the Rights Holder

Verify that the person granting permission controls the relevant rights or is authorised to act on behalf of the rights holder.

### 17.2 Describe the Material

Identify the exact work, excerpt, image, figure, recording, dataset, or other material.

### 17.3 Describe the Intended Use

Explain:

- The publication.
- The article or project.
- The amount of material to be used.
- Whether translation is involved.
- Whether adaptation is involved.
- Whether the material will be hosted online.
- Whether it may appear in feeds or social-media previews.
- Whether it will be distributed in downloadable formats.
- Whether the publication is commercial or non-commercial, where relevant.

### 17.4 Request Specific Permissions

Avoid vague requests such as "permission to use this material."

Specify the rights needed.

### 17.5 Record the Response

Retain:

- Request date.
- Rights-holder identity.
- Response date.
- Exact permission granted.
- Conditions.
- Duration.
- Relevant correspondence.
- Any subsequent amendments.

### 17.6 No Response

Silence should not be interpreted as permission unless an independently established legal basis applies.

If permission is necessary and cannot be obtained, choose another lawful approach or exclude the material.

## 18. Rights Status and Publication Decisions

Use consistent status labels.

| Status | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Verified and approved | Rights basis and intended use have been checked |
| Licensed | Use is governed by an identified licence |
| Permission granted | Appropriate authorisation has been recorded |
| Original project material | Ownership or contribution rights have been established |
| Public domain assessed | Public-domain basis has been examined |
| Restricted | Use is permitted only under specified conditions |
| Pending | Additional verification or permission is required |
| Unclear | Rights status cannot yet be established |
| Disputed | Ownership, authorisation, or permitted use is contested |
| Expired | A time-limited permission or licence has ended |
| Revoked | An applicable permission has been withdrawn, subject to its terms and applicable law |
| Not approved | The proposed use is not authorised or otherwise sufficiently established |
| Removed | Material has been removed from publication |

### 18.1 Pending Material

Material with pending, unclear, or disputed rights should not be published in a manner that assumes unrestricted permission.

### 18.2 Restricted Material

Clearly document the restrictions and ensure that the proposed publication remains within them.

### 18.3 Expired or Revoked Permission

Review affected material promptly.

Consider whether continued use remains lawful and whether removal, replacement, or further permission is necessary.

### 18.4 No Status by Assumption

A blank field must never be interpreted as approval.

## 19. Publication and Distribution Channels

Rights should be assessed against the actual distribution methods used by the publication.

Potential channels include:

- Main website.
- Individual article pages.
- Weekly collection pages.
- RSS feeds.
- XML feeds.
- Downloadable documents.
- Social-media previews.
- Search-engine indexing.
- Public source-code repositories.
- Archive copies.
- Other authorised publication formats.

Permission to display material on one platform may not automatically authorise every other use.

Particular attention should be paid to full-text syndication, downloadable copies, social-media uploads, and redistribution.

## 20. Attribution and Credit Management

Maintain consistent attribution wherever third-party material is used.

### 20.1 Article-Level Credit

Relevant credits should appear close to the material where practical.

Examples include:

- Image captions.
- Figure notes.
- Literary source details.
- Translator credits.
- Audio and video credits.
- Dataset citations.

### 20.2 Central Asset Credits

The publication should also maintain an appropriate central asset record in `ASSET_CREDITS.md`.

### 20.3 Attribution Accuracy

Do not:

- Invent creators.
- Misidentify a translator.
- Attribute a recording to the wrong performer.
- Omit a required licence reference.
- Attribute a third-party image to the publication.
- Imply endorsement through a credit line.

### 20.4 Changes

Where an asset is materially modified, record the modification as required by the applicable licence or permission.

## 21. Managing Permissions and Evidence

Permission records are important editorial evidence.

### 21.1 Evidence Types

Appropriate evidence may include:

- Signed permission letters.
- Written email authorisations.
- Formal licence documents.
- Contributor agreements.
- Publisher permission statements.
- Official repository licence pages.
- Archived licence records.
- Public-domain research notes.
- Other relevant documentation.

### 21.2 Evidence Integrity

Retain evidence in a form that allows its origin and relevant terms to be understood.

Do not rewrite a permission message in a way that changes its meaning.

### 21.3 Secure Storage

Private permission correspondence may contain personal details or contractual terms.

Store these records securely.

### 21.4 Internal References

Use an internal evidence reference instead of publishing private correspondence.

Example:

`VV-RP-2026-001-E01`

### 21.5 Public Documentation

Where appropriate, publish the licence reference, required attribution, or other non-confidential rights information.

Do not expose personal contact information or confidential agreements unnecessarily.

## 22. Rights Review During Publication

Rights clearance should be checked before publication.

It should not be postponed until a complaint is received.

### 22.1 Initial Review

Identify all third-party material during article preparation.

### 22.2 Pre-Publication Review

Verify the intended use, required permissions, relevant licences, and attribution.

### 22.3 Final Review

Check that the final published article uses the material in the approved form.

### 22.4 Changes After Approval

Where a figure, quotation, image, translation, or other asset is replaced or modified, reassess its rights status.

### 22.5 Reuse in Other Formats

Do not assume that permission for the original article automatically covers later republication, commercial use, promotional materials, or external distribution.

## 23. Rights Disputes and Takedown Requests

Any credible rights concern should be assessed promptly.

Possible issues include:

- Incorrect ownership attribution.
- Unauthorised reproduction.
- Exceeding licence conditions.
- Expired permission.
- Disputed ownership.
- Incorrect public-domain assessment.
- Unauthorised translation.
- Unauthorised modification.
- Infringement allegations.
- Misuse of confidential or restricted material.

### 23.1 Immediate Assessment

Identify the affected material, article, permission record, and nature of the claim.

### 23.2 Temporary Restriction

Where there is a credible risk of infringement or significant harm, temporary restriction or removal may be appropriate while the matter is examined.

### 23.3 Verification

Check the original source, licence, permission record, and relevant correspondence.

### 23.4 Resolution

Possible outcomes include:

- No change, with an explanation.
- Corrected attribution.
- Additional permission.
- Replacement with another asset.
- Removal of affected material.
- Correction of the article.
- Partial withdrawal.
- Full withdrawal.

### 23.5 Record

Document the decision and any resulting changes.

Refer to `TAKEDOWN_POLICY.md` and `CORRECTIONS_POLICY.md`.

## 24. Recommended Register Maintenance

The master register should be reviewed periodically.

### 24.1 Review Triggers

A new review may be necessary when:

- An article is substantially revised.
- New material is added.
- A permission is changed.
- A licence version changes.
- A rights holder raises a concern.
- A third-party platform changes its terms.
- A public-domain assessment is questioned.
- The material is reused in another format.
- The relevant distribution method changes.

### 24.2 Responsible Person

Each record should identify the editor or other responsible person who completed or reviewed the assessment.

### 24.3 Review Frequency

Periodic checks should be proportionate to the volume and sensitivity of the material.

### 24.4 Version History

Preserve significant changes to rights decisions.

Do not silently remove an earlier restriction or overwrite evidence of a previous permission arrangement.

## 29. Continuing Commitment

An archive is not built responsibly by collecting material without regard to its origin.

Every poem, translation, image, recording, dataset, research figure, and other contribution has a history of creation, ownership, permission, or cultural association.

Verse & Variable seeks to respect those histories.

The purpose of this register is not merely to prevent disputes. It is to ensure that the publication recognises intellectual contribution, respects legitimate rights, and makes informed decisions about what it reproduces and distributes.

**Use only what can be responsibly used. Credit those who created it. Preserve the conditions under which it was shared.**
