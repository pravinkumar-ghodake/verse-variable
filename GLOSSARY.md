
# Glossary

**Verse & Variable**  
*A line of verse. A line of inquiry.*  
**لفظ سے لہر تک — Lafz se Lehar tak**

## 1. Purpose

This glossary provides concise explanations of literary, linguistic, philosophical, and scientific terminology used throughout Verse & Variable.

The publication brings together traditions that have developed their own vocabularies, conceptual frameworks, and technical languages. A shared glossary helps readers move between these areas while preserving the distinctions that make each discipline meaningful.

The glossary is intended to:

- Explain unfamiliar literary and cultural terms.
- Clarify linguistic and translation terminology.
- Introduce philosophical concepts used in commentary.
- Explain scientific vocabulary relevant to the publication.
- Distinguish related concepts that are frequently confused.
- Establish consistent terminology across articles.
- Support readers approaching subjects outside their specialist background.

It is a developing reference, not an exhaustive dictionary or a substitute for specialist research.

## 2. How to Use This Glossary

Entries are organised by subject area rather than strictly alphabetically.

Some terms appear in more than one discipline. Their meaning may vary according to context.

For example, *resonance* has technical meanings in physics and engineering, but it is also commonly used as a literary or emotional metaphor.

When a term is used in a technical sense in an article, its meaning should be established by the relevant scientific context and sources.

The definitions below are introductory. More specialised or mathematically precise explanations should be supported by appropriate references in individual articles.

# Part I — Literary Forms and Traditions

## 3. General Literary Terms

**Aesthetic**  
Relating to the perception, experience, and appreciation of beauty, artistic form, and expressive qualities.

**Alliteration**  
The repetition of similar initial consonant sounds in nearby words, often used to create rhythm, emphasis, or sound patterns.

**Allusion**  
An indirect reference to another literary work, historical event, cultural tradition, person, or idea.

**Allegory**  
A narrative or expression in which characters, events, or images carry meanings beyond their immediate presentation, often through an extended symbolic structure.

**Ballad**  
A narrative form of poetry or song that tells a story, frequently through repeated structures or musical phrasing.

**Couplet**  
A pair of successive poetic lines, often connected through rhyme, rhythm, or meaning.

**Elegy**  
A literary expression of mourning, loss, remembrance, or reflection, particularly associated with death.

**Enjambment**  
The continuation of a sentence or grammatical unit beyond the end of a poetic line without a complete syntactic pause.

**Free verse**  
Poetry that does not follow a fixed metrical or rhyme scheme, although it may still use rhythm, repetition, sound, and deliberate line structure.

**Imagery**  
Language that evokes sensory experience, mental pictures, feelings, or associations.

**Lyric poetry**  
Poetry that expresses an individual's thoughts, emotions, perceptions, or experiences rather than primarily narrating an extended story.

**Metaphor**  
A figure of speech in which one thing is described through another to express a perceived relationship or quality.

**Motif**  
A recurring image, idea, symbol, phrase, or element that contributes to a work's structure or meaning.

**Narrative**  
The structured presentation of events, actions, or experiences as a story.

**Personification**  
A figure of speech that attributes human characteristics or actions to non-human entities, objects, or abstract ideas.

**Refrain**  
A line, phrase, or group of lines repeated at intervals within a poem or song.

**Rhyme**  
A correspondence of sounds, usually at the ends of words or lines, used as a poetic or musical device.

**Symbolism**  
The use of an object, image, action, or expression to represent an idea or meaning beyond its immediate literal sense.

**Tone**  
The attitude or emotional character conveyed through a literary expression.

**Verse**  
A line or unit of poetry; the word can also refer more broadly to metrical or poetic writing.

## 4. Urdu and Persianate Literary Terminology

**Beher / Bahr (بحر)**  
The metrical pattern or quantitative rhythmic framework used in classical Urdu and Persian poetry. Different metres follow different arrangements of long and short syllabic values.

**Ghazal (غزل)**  
A poetic form commonly organised into autonomous or interconnected couplets, traditionally following a shared rhyme and refrain pattern and a consistent metre. The form has developed across Persian, Urdu, and other literary traditions.

**Matla (مطلع)**  
The opening couplet of a conventional ghazal, in which both lines establish the rhyme and refrain pattern.

**Maqta (مقطع)**  
The concluding couplet of a ghazal. It often contains the poet's pen name, or *takhallus*, although this is not compulsory in every ghazal.

**Misra (مصرع)**  
One line or hemistich of a poetic couplet. A conventional sher consists of two misras.

**Nazm (نظم)**  
A broad Urdu poetic form that generally develops an extended subject, theme, or argument. Its structure may be metrical, rhymed, or free verse.

**Qafiya (قافیہ)**  
The rhyme element that appears before the recurring refrain in a traditional ghazal structure.

**Qasida (قصیدہ)**  
A long-form poem traditionally associated with praise, panegyric, celebration, satire, or other extended subjects.

**Radif (ردیف)**  
The recurring word or phrase repeated after the qafiya in a ghazal or another relevant poetic structure.

**Rubai (رباعی)**  
A four-line poetic form traditionally associated with a distinctive metrical structure and often a philosophical, reflective, or contemplative subject.

**Sher (شعر)**  
A poetic couplet consisting of two misras. In a ghazal, each sher frequently expresses a relatively complete thought or image, even when it participates in the work's wider thematic atmosphere.

**Takhallus (تخلص)**  
The pen name or poetic signature adopted by a poet, often appearing in the maqta of a ghazal.

## 5. Hindi and North Indian Literary Terminology

**Bhajan (भजन)**  
A devotional song or composition expressing reverence, devotion, remembrance, or spiritual reflection.

**Chaupai (चौपाई)**  
A poetic form consisting of four-part units or lines, associated with particular metrical conventions in North Indian literary traditions.

**Doha (दोहा)**  
A traditional North Indian couplet form, commonly associated with a distinctive metrical pattern and concise philosophical, ethical, devotional, or observational expression.

**Kavita (कविता)**  
The Hindi term for poetry or a poem.

**Lok Sahitya (लोक साहित्य)**  
Folk literature: expressions, narratives, songs, and traditions developed and transmitted within communities.

**Muktak (मुक्तक)**  
An independent poetic composition that can be understood as a complete unit without depending on a larger sequence.

## 6. Marathi Literary Terminology

**Abhang (अभंग)**  
A devotional poetic form strongly associated with the Marathi Varkari tradition and poets such as the medieval Marathi saint-poets. Abhangs often express devotion, spiritual reflection, social observation, and personal experience.

**Bhakti (भक्ती)**  
A broad tradition of devotion and personal relationship with the divine, expressed through poetry, song, philosophical reflection, and religious practice across several Indian traditions.

**Ovi (ओवी)**  
A traditional Marathi poetic form used in several literary and folk contexts, including narrative and devotional expression.

**Powada (पोवाडा)**  
A Marathi narrative and performative ballad traditionally associated with heroic deeds, historical events, and remembrance.

**Varkari tradition (वारकरी संप्रदाय)**  
A devotional movement and pilgrimage tradition associated particularly with the worship of Vithoba at Pandharpur and the Marathi bhakti literary heritage.

## 7. Kannada Literary Terminology

**Bhavageethe (ಭಾವಗೀತೆ)**  
A form of Kannada lyrical poetry or song centred on emotional expression, reflection, and personal experience.

**Tripadi (ತ್ರಿಪದಿ)**  
A traditional Kannada poetic form organised into three lines, associated with characteristic rhythmic and structural conventions.

**Vachana (ವಚನ)**  
A distinctive Kannada prose-poetic devotional and philosophical expression, particularly associated with the Virashaiva or Lingayat movement and its historical literary tradition.

## 8. Tamil Literary Terminology

**Akam (அகம்)**  
A major conceptual category in classical Tamil poetics associated with interior experience, especially love, emotion, relationships, and personal life.

**Puram (புறம்)**  
A major conceptual category in classical Tamil poetics associated with public life, including war, heroism, generosity, kingship, ethics, and social experience.

**Sangam literature (சங்க இலக்கியம்)**  
A body of classical Tamil literature associated with the early Tamil literary corpus and its poetic and cultural traditions.

**Thirukkural (திருக்குறள்)**  
A celebrated classical Tamil work composed of concise couplets addressing ethical conduct, social life, governance, and human experience.

## 9. Malayalam Literary Terminology

**Kilippattu (കിളിപ്പാട്ട്)**  
A Malayalam literary form in which a bird or another speaking figure may serve as a narrative or introductory voice. The form is especially associated with certain devotional and narrative compositions.

**Manipravalam (മണിപ്രവാളം)**  
A historical literary style involving a blend of Malayalam and Sanskrit linguistic elements.

**Pattu (പാട്ട്)**  
A general Malayalam term for song, with broader literary and traditional uses depending on context.

## 10. Telugu Literary Terminology

**Dwipada (ద్విపద)**  
A Telugu poetic form organised in couplets, associated with narrative and other literary traditions.

**Padyam (పద్యం)**  
A general term for a verse or metrical poetic composition in Telugu.

**Satakam (శతకం)**  
A poetic collection conventionally consisting of approximately one hundred verses, often organised around a common devotional, ethical, philosophical, or thematic focus.

# Part II — Language and Translation

## 11. Linguistic Terminology

**Accent**  
A characteristic pattern of pronunciation associated with an individual, region, community, or language variety.

**Code-switching**  
The use of more than one language or language variety within a conversation or expression.

**Connotation**  
The associations, implications, or emotional meanings attached to a word beyond its basic denotative meaning.

**Denotation**  
The direct or conventional meaning of a word or expression.

**Dialect**  
A language variety distinguished by features such as vocabulary, grammar, pronunciation, or usage, often associated with a geographical or social community.

**Etymology**  
The study of the historical origin and development of words.

**Idiom**  
An established expression whose meaning cannot always be inferred directly from the meanings of its individual words.

**Lexicon**  
The vocabulary of a language, individual, discipline, or field of study.

**Morphology**  
The branch of linguistics concerned with the internal structure and formation of words.

**Phoneme**  
A unit of sound that can distinguish meaning in a particular language.

**Phonetics**  
The study of the physical production, transmission, and perception of speech sounds.

**Polysemy**  
The condition in which one word has multiple related meanings.

**Register**  
A variety of language selected according to context, purpose, audience, or social situation.

**Semantics**  
The study of meaning in language and linguistic expressions.

**Syntax**  
The rules and structures governing how words and phrases combine to form sentences.

**Transcription**  
The representation of spoken language or recorded speech in written form. It may capture pronunciation more directly than ordinary spelling.

**Transliteration**  
The conversion of written text from one script into another, generally according to an identifiable correspondence between writing systems.

**Unicode**  
A universal character-encoding standard that provides a consistent framework for representing characters from many writing systems.

## 12. Translation Terminology

**Adaptation**  
A reworking of an original text that may change wording, structure, imagery, or presentation to serve a particular purpose or audience.

**Close translation**  
A translation that prioritises the source wording and structure while aiming to preserve its meaning.

**Cultural equivalence**  
A translation approach that considers how cultural expressions and references can be communicated meaningfully to readers in another linguistic context.

**Free translation**  
A translation that prioritises overall meaning and readability rather than close structural correspondence with the original.

**Literal translation**  
A translation that closely follows the direct wording or grammatical structure of the original. Literal translation is not automatically accurate when idioms or figurative expressions are involved.

**Machine translation**  
The use of computational systems to translate text or speech between languages.

**Source language**  
The language from which a translation is made.

**Target language**  
The language into which a translation is made.

**Translator's note**  
An explanatory note identifying a significant linguistic, cultural, historical, or interpretative decision made during translation.

**Untranslatability**  
A term used to describe expressions whose full meaning, form, or cultural significance cannot be reproduced directly in another language. It does not mean that useful translation is impossible.

# Part III — Philosophy and Intellectual Traditions

## 13. Philosophical Concepts

**Aesthetics**  
The branch of philosophy concerned with beauty, art, taste, perception, and aesthetic experience.

**Dialectic**  
A method of philosophical inquiry or reasoning that examines positions, contradictions, and their possible resolution or development. Its precise meaning varies across philosophical traditions.

**Epistemology**  
The branch of philosophy concerned with knowledge, belief, justification, evidence, and the limits of knowing.

**Ethics**  
The branch of philosophy concerned with questions of right and wrong, responsibility, values, and moral conduct.

**Existentialism**  
A broad philosophical movement concerned with existence, freedom, choice, responsibility, and the individual's relationship with the world.

**Hermeneutics**  
The theory and practice of interpretation, particularly of texts, language, historical material, and cultural expressions.

**Metaphysics**  
The branch of philosophy concerned with fundamental questions about reality, existence, identity, causation, and the nature of things.

**Ontology**  
The study of being and existence, including questions about what kinds of things exist and how they are related.

**Phenomenology**  
A philosophical approach concerned with the structures of experience and how phenomena appear to consciousness.

**Rationalism**  
A family of philosophical positions that gives reason an important role in the acquisition or justification of knowledge.

**Relativism**  
A family of positions according to which certain truths, values, or standards depend on a framework, perspective, or context.

**Stoicism**  
An ancient Greek and Roman philosophical tradition concerned with virtue, reason, self-governance, and the distinction between what is and is not within one's control.

## 14. Indian Aesthetic and Philosophical Concepts

**Advaita (अद्वैत)**  
A philosophical position meaning non-duality, associated especially with Advaita Vedanta. Its precise interpretation requires attention to the relevant school and texts.

**Alankara (अलंकार)**  
In Indian poetics, a figure or ornament of literary expression, such as a form of metaphor, comparison, or other rhetorical device.

**Dharma (धर्म)**  
A context-dependent concept with meanings that may include duty, ethical order, law, conduct, teaching, or sustaining principle across different Indian traditions.

**Dhvani (ध्वनि)**  
In classical Indian literary theory, the suggested or implied meaning of a literary expression beyond its direct or literal sense.

**Karma (कर्म)**  
A concept concerning action and its consequences in several Indian philosophical and religious traditions. Its specific meaning varies considerably across those traditions.

**Moksha (मोक्ष)**  
A term for liberation or release, particularly from cycles of rebirth in several Indian philosophical and religious traditions.

**Rasa (रस)**  
A central concept in Indian aesthetics concerning the aesthetic experience or flavour evoked through artistic expression.

**Samsara (संसार)**  
The cycle of birth, death, and rebirth in several Indian philosophical and religious traditions.

**Vairagya (वैराग्य)**  
A concept associated with detachment or dispassion, especially in relation to attachment and worldly desires.

**Yoga (योग)**  
A broad term covering philosophical, contemplative, ethical, and practical traditions. Its meaning depends on the school and context.

These terms should not be treated as interchangeable or assumed to have identical meanings across different Indian philosophical traditions.

# Part IV — Physics, Mechanics, and Wave Phenomena

## 15. Fundamental Wave and Oscillation Terminology

**Amplitude**  
A measure of the maximum magnitude of a displacement or oscillating quantity relative to a chosen reference or equilibrium position.

**Attenuation**  
The reduction in the amplitude or intensity of a wave as it propagates through a medium or system.

**Dispersion**  
The dependence of wave propagation characteristics, such as phase velocity or group velocity, on frequency or wavelength.

**Frequency**  
The number of cycles or oscillations per unit time, generally measured in hertz (Hz).

**Group velocity**  
The velocity at which the envelope of a wave packet or group of components propagates under the relevant conditions.

**Harmonic**  
A frequency component related to a fundamental frequency by an integer multiple. The precise definition and relevance depend on the system being studied.

**Oscillation**  
A repeated variation of a quantity around an equilibrium or reference state.

**Phase**  
The position of an oscillation within its cycle, often expressed as an angle or a fraction of a period.

**Phase velocity**  
The velocity at which a surface or point of constant phase propagates.

**Resonance**  
A condition in which a system responds strongly to excitation near one of its natural frequencies or characteristic modes, subject to damping and other system properties.

**Superposition**  
The principle that, in a linear system, the combined response to multiple inputs equals the sum of the responses to the individual inputs.

**Wavelength**  
The spatial period of a propagating wave, commonly measured as the distance between successive points of equal phase.

## 16. Nonlinear Dynamics and Mechanics

**Constitutive relation**  
A mathematical relationship describing how a material responds to physical quantities such as stress, strain, temperature, or other relevant state variables.

**Contact nonlinearity**  
Nonlinear behaviour arising from interactions between contacting bodies or surfaces, potentially involving opening, closing, friction, impacts, or changing contact stiffness.

**Hysteresis**  
A dependence of a system's current response on its previous state or loading history. The response during loading may differ from the response during unloading.

**Linear system**  
A system whose governing behaviour satisfies linearity, including the superposition principle under the relevant mathematical conditions.

**Nonlinear system**  
A system whose governing relationships do not satisfy linearity. Its response may depend on amplitude, state, interactions, or other variables in ways that cannot be described by simple superposition.

**Nonlinear elasticity**  
The study of elastic material behaviour in which the stress-strain relationship or governing mechanics require nonlinear descriptions.

**Nonlinear vibration**  
Oscillatory behaviour governed by nonlinear equations or restoring forces, potentially producing amplitude-dependent frequencies, bifurcations, harmonics, and other complex responses.

**Nonlinear wave**  
A wave whose propagation is influenced by nonlinear effects in the governing medium or system.

**Strain**  
A measure of deformation, commonly expressed as a change in length or geometry relative to an appropriate reference configuration.

**Stress**  
A measure of internal force intensity within a material, commonly represented by a stress tensor in continuum mechanics.

## 17. Nonlinear Acoustics and Harmonic Phenomena

**Frequency mixing**  
The production of new frequency components through interactions between waves or signals in a nonlinear system.

**Fundamental frequency**  
The primary or reference frequency of an oscillatory signal or periodic waveform.

**Harmonic generation**  
The production of frequency components related to an input or fundamental frequency through nonlinear processes.

**Higher harmonics**  
Harmonic components at integer multiples of a fundamental frequency above the fundamental itself.

**Nonlinear acoustics**  
The study of acoustic wave behaviour in which nonlinear effects influence propagation, waveform evolution, interactions, or scattering.

**Nonlinear ultrasonic testing**  
A family of ultrasonic evaluation methods that use nonlinear acoustic responses, such as harmonic generation or wave interaction, to investigate material condition and defects.

**Second-harmonic generation (SHG)**  
The generation of a component at twice a fundamental frequency through a nonlinear interaction. The physical mechanism depends on the system.

**Third-harmonic generation (THG)**  
The generation of a component at three times a fundamental frequency through a nonlinear process.

**Sum-frequency generation**  
A nonlinear interaction in which two frequency components produce a new component at the sum of their frequencies.

**Difference-frequency generation**  
A nonlinear interaction in which two frequency components produce a new component at the absolute difference between their frequencies, under appropriate interaction and frequency conditions.

**Wave mixing**  
A nonlinear interaction between two or more waves that produces modified or additional frequency components or other coupled responses.

## 18. Metamaterials and Wave Control

**Bandgap**  
A frequency range in which wave propagation is strongly inhibited or restricted in a structure or medium, subject to its configuration and boundary conditions.

**Bragg scattering**  
Wave scattering associated with periodic spatial variations, where interference can produce strong frequency-dependent propagation effects.

**Effective medium**  
A simplified description in which a complex material or structure is represented by averaged or effective properties over an appropriate scale.

**Mechanical metamaterial**  
An engineered structure designed to exhibit mechanical properties or wave behaviour that arise from its architecture rather than solely from the properties of its constituent materials.

**Phononic crystal**  
A periodically structured medium designed to control elastic or acoustic wave propagation, often through band formation and Bragg scattering.

**Phononic metamaterial**  
An engineered material or structure designed to produce unusual or tailored elastic and acoustic responses, potentially including effective properties and local-resonance effects.

**Local resonance**  
A wave-control mechanism in which local resonant elements interact with propagating waves and can produce frequency-dependent effective behaviour.

**Nonlinear metamaterial**  
A metamaterial whose designed response includes nonlinear behaviour, potentially enabling amplitude-dependent wave propagation, frequency conversion, or other nonlinear phenomena.

**Wave manipulation**  
The deliberate control of wave propagation, scattering, transmission, reflection, focusing, or other wave behaviour through material design or external modulation.

**Wave scattering**  
The redirection or redistribution of wave energy due to an object, interface, inhomogeneity, or change in the propagation environment.

## 19. Time-Dependent and Time-Periodic Systems

**Floquet theory**  
A mathematical framework for analysing systems with periodic coefficients or time-periodic dynamics. It is widely used in the study of periodically driven systems.

**Frequency conversion**  
A process in which energy or signal content is transferred between different frequency components through a physical interaction or modulation.

**Parametric modulation**  
The deliberate or naturally occurring variation of one or more system parameters, potentially changing the system's response.

**Spatiotemporal modulation**  
A variation of system properties across both space and time.

**Temporal modulation**  
A change in material or system properties over time.

**Time-periodic system**  
A system whose relevant parameters or governing properties repeat periodically in time.

**Time crystal**  
A physical phase or system displaying a form of temporal order associated with periodic behaviour. Different theoretical and experimental settings use different definitions; a driven discrete time crystal, for example, involves a response with a period related to, but distinct from, the driving period. A time-modulated material is not automatically a time crystal.

**Time-modulated metamaterial**  
An engineered material or structure whose effective properties are deliberately varied over time to control wave behaviour.

## 20. Cloaking and Inverse Design

**Cloaking**  
A broad term for strategies intended to reduce, redirect, or control the scattering or detection of an object or region by waves. The achievable effect depends on the physical mechanism, frequency range, geometry, and implementation.

**Inverse design**  
An approach in which desired system behaviour is specified first and the material, geometry, parameters, or structure are then determined through optimisation or other design methods.

**Inverse problem**  
A problem in which unknown properties, parameters, or causes are inferred from observed responses or data.

**Forward problem**  
A problem in which system properties, inputs, and governing equations are used to predict the resulting response.

**Bayesian inversion**  
An inverse-problem approach that combines observed data with prior information through a probabilistic framework to estimate unknown quantities and their uncertainty.

**Scattering cancellation**  
A strategy intended to reduce scattering by arranging interactions that suppress or counteract selected scattered fields under particular conditions.

# Part V — Computational Mechanics and Experimental Methods

## 21. Computational and Mathematical Terminology

**Boundary condition**  
A mathematical or physical condition imposed at the boundary of a model or domain.

**Convergence**  
The tendency of a numerical method, iterative process, or sequence of approximations to approach a stable or limiting result.

**Finite element analysis (FEA)**  
The computational analysis of a physical or mathematical problem using the finite element method.

**Finite element method (FEM)**  
A numerical method that divides a domain into smaller elements and approximates the governing problem through discrete mathematical formulations.

**Initial condition**  
A condition specifying the state of a system at the beginning of a time-dependent problem.

**Mesh**  
A discretisation of a computational domain into elements, cells, or other smaller units used for numerical analysis.

**Numerical model**  
A mathematical or computational representation used to approximate the behaviour of a system.

**Parameter estimation**  
The process of determining unknown model parameters from observations, experiments, or other information.

**Sensitivity analysis**  
The study of how variations in inputs, parameters, or assumptions affect the outputs of a model.

**Simulation**  
The computational execution of a model to examine predicted behaviour under specified conditions.

**Uncertainty quantification**  
The evaluation and representation of uncertainty in model inputs, parameters, predictions, or results.

**Validation**  
The assessment of whether a model represents the relevant real-world system adequately for its intended use.

**Verification**  
The assessment of whether a computational model or implementation correctly solves the mathematical formulation it is intended to represent.

## 22. Materials and Modelling

**Elasticity**  
The study of how materials deform under applied forces and recover their shape when the forces are removed, within the relevant elastic regime.

**Hyperelastic material**  
An idealised material model in which stress is derived from a strain-energy function, often used to represent particular forms of finite-strain elastic behaviour.

**Murnaghan model**  
A nonlinear elastic constitutive model used to describe material response through higher-order elastic constants. Its precise formulation depends on the chosen strain and energy representation.

**Material parameter**  
A quantity used to describe a material's properties within a mathematical or constitutive model.

**Strain-energy density**  
The elastic energy stored per unit volume, expressed as a function of the deformation or strain measures used in a material model.

## 23. Nondestructive Evaluation and Structural Monitoring

**Nondestructive evaluation (NDE)**  
The examination of a material, component, or structure to obtain information about its condition without causing unacceptable damage.

**Nondestructive testing (NDT)**  
Testing methods used to identify or assess material and structural characteristics without destroying the item being examined. The terms NDT and NDE are often used closely, although their emphasis can differ by context.

**Structural health monitoring (SHM)**  
The use of sensing, data analysis, and diagnostic methods to assess the condition or performance of structures over time.

**Guided wave**  
A wave that propagates while being constrained or guided by a structure or interface, such as a plate, shell, or pipe.

**Lamb wave**  
An elastic guided-wave mode that propagates in a plate or similar bounded structure, involving coupled displacement components and frequency-dependent mode behaviour.

**Defect detection**  
The identification of a discontinuity, flaw, or abnormality in a material or structure using suitable inspection methods.

# Part VI — Research and Evidence

## 24. Research Terminology

**Citation**  
A reference that identifies the source supporting a statement, quotation, idea, or piece of information.

**DOI (Digital Object Identifier)**  
A persistent identifier used to identify digital publications and other registered objects.

**Experimental evidence**  
Evidence obtained through observation or measurement in an experiment.

**Hypothesis**  
A proposed explanation or proposition that can be examined through appropriate evidence or investigation.

**Peer review**  
A process in which scholarly work is evaluated by other researchers or qualified reviewers before or after publication, depending on the publication system.

**Preprint**  
A scholarly manuscript shared publicly before formal journal publication or completion of peer review.

**Primary source**  
An original document, work, record, dataset, or study directly relevant to the subject being investigated.

**Replication**  
The repetition of a study or experiment to examine whether its findings can be reproduced under specified conditions.

**Review article**  
A scholarly publication that examines, synthesises, or evaluates existing research on a subject.

**Scholarly attribution**  
The accurate identification of the people, publications, institutions, or intellectual contributions associated with a work or claim.

**Source verification**  
The process of checking a source's identity, authenticity, accuracy, relevance, and suitability for supporting a particular claim.

# Part VII — Editorial Distinctions

## 25. Terms That Should Not Be Confused

The following distinctions are especially important to the interdisciplinary identity of Verse & Variable.

| Term A | Term B | Important distinction |
|---|---|---|
| Translation | Transliteration | Translation communicates meaning in another language; transliteration represents writing through another script. |
| Transcription | Transliteration | Transcription records spoken language or pronunciation; transliteration maps written forms between scripts. |
| Poet | Performer | A poet creates a literary work; a performer presents or interprets it. One person may hold both roles, but they are not automatically identical. |
| Lyricist | Singer | The lyricist writes the lyrics; the singer performs them. |
| Metaphor | Mechanism | A metaphor creates an expressive or conceptual comparison; a mechanism describes how a process physically occurs. |
| Analogy | Equivalence | An analogy highlights a meaningful similarity; equivalence asserts a stronger relationship that requires justification. |
| Hypothesis | Established result | A hypothesis proposes something to be examined; an established result has supporting evidence appropriate to its claim. |
| Simulation | Experiment | A simulation calculates the behaviour of a model; an experiment investigates a physical system through observation or measurement. |
| Verification | Validation | Verification examines whether the model is solved correctly; validation examines whether it represents the intended real-world system adequately. |
| Phononic crystal | Phononic metamaterial | Both can control wave propagation, but periodicity and engineered effective behaviour are distinct design considerations and the terms are not universally interchangeable. |
| Time modulation | Time crystal | Time modulation describes changing system properties; a time crystal refers to a more specific form of temporal order. |
| Nonlinearity | Complexity | Nonlinearity means governing relationships are not linear; complexity may involve many interacting factors and can occur in linear or nonlinear systems. |
| NDT | SHM | NDT refers to examination methods; SHM concerns monitoring structural condition over time. |
| Original work | Published translation | A translation is a separate linguistic rendering and may carry independent authorship and rights. |

## 26. Usage in Published Articles

The glossary provides introductory definitions, but individual articles must use terminology according to the relevant subject and context.

When a term is particularly important to an article:

- Define it in the article if its meaning is not obvious.
- Cite an authoritative reference when a technical or disputed claim requires support.
- Explain specialised terminology for non-specialist readers.
- Distinguish an established meaning from an interpretative use.
- Avoid using technical terminology merely for stylistic effect.

Where a term has several accepted meanings, identify the intended meaning.

A metaphorical use of a scientific term should remain distinguishable from its technical use.

## 27. Expansion and Maintenance

This glossary will expand as the publication develops.

New entries may be added when:

- A literary tradition introduces terminology not yet covered.
- A language-specific concept requires explanation.
- A philosophical term becomes important to an article.
- A scientific subject receives sustained coverage.

- A term is repeatedly used across several articles.
