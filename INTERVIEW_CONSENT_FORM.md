## 1. About This Form

This form records the consent and permissions of an individual participating in an interview, recorded conversation, or oral-history contribution intended for possible use by Verse & Variable.

The publication explores literature, language, culture, history, philosophy, and scientific inquiry. Interviews may involve personal experiences, professional knowledge, literary interpretation, historical recollections, or specialist perspectives.

Participation is voluntary.

Please read this form before agreeing to participate. You may ask questions, request clarification, decline individual permissions, or decline the interview altogether.

This is a general editorial consent template. It must be adapted to the interview, its risks, the participant's circumstances, and any applicable legal or institutional requirements. It is not a substitute for a formal consent document required by a research institution, ethics committee, regulator, or other competent authority.

- The editor may make limited readability adjustments without changing the intended meaning.
- Selected portions may be omitted.
- Substantial editing or reconstruction should be identified where necessary.
- My words should not be deliberately altered to misrepresent my position.
- My participation does not automatically give me editorial control over the entire article.
- My participation does not mean that I endorse every argument or interpretation in the article.

## 12. Privacy and Personal Information

The interview may involve personal information such as your name, professional background, contact details, voice, image, experiences, or statements.

Verse & Variable will seek to collect and use only information reasonably necessary for the agreed editorial purpose.

The publication will take reasonable measures to:

- Avoid unnecessary collection of personal information.
- Limit access to sensitive interview records.
- Protect confidential material.
- Use agreed attribution.
- Avoid unnecessary disclosure of private details.
- Handle corrections and concerns responsibly.

Please do not provide passwords, financial account credentials, government identification numbers, or other unnecessary sensitive identifiers during the interview.

### 12.1 Sensitive Information

Where the interview may involve health information, family matters, financial circumstances, political or religious beliefs, personal trauma, allegations, or other sensitive subjects, the interviewer should explain the intended use and consider whether additional safeguards are necessary.

You may decline to discuss such matters.

### 12.2 Third-Party Information

Please avoid sharing private information about other individuals unless it is necessary, appropriate, and supported by a legitimate basis.

Your permission does not automatically authorise publication of another person's confidential or personal information.

## 13. Confidentiality

The editor will handle material in accordance with the agreed permissions and the publication's relevant policies.

However, confidentiality has practical and legal limitations.

These may include:

- Information already available publicly.
- Material independently disclosed by other sources.
- Third-party records or copies.
- Legal disclosure obligations.
- Backups or external service retention.
- Information that makes a person identifiable indirectly.

The publication should explain any particular limitation before obtaining consent where it is foreseeable and relevant.

Do not assume that an anonymous attribution guarantees that the underlying information can never be associated with you.

## 16. Withdrawal and Changes to Consent

You may contact the editor to raise concerns about your participation, identification, quotations, privacy, or the proposed use of your contribution.

### 16.1 Before Publication

Where a withdrawal request is received before publication, the editor will assess the request in light of the agreed permissions and applicable obligations.

Where appropriate, proposed use of the affected material may be suspended while the request is considered.

### 16.2 After Publication

After publication, the editor will consider whether correction, anonymisation, redaction, restriction, or removal is appropriate.

The response may depend on the circumstances, including the nature of the consent, the concern raised, the material already published, and relevant legal obligations.

### 16.3 Third-Party Copies

Removal from the publication website cannot guarantee removal from:

- Search-engine indexes.
- Archived copies.
- Backups.
- Third-party websites.
- Previously downloaded documents.
- Copies independently reproduced by others.

### 16.4 Changing Permissions

Where practical, a participant may contact the editor to discuss a proposed change in permissions.

The editor should not treat a change request as automatically invalid merely because the original permission was granted.
