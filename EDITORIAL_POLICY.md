## 1. Purpose and Editorial Identity

Verse & Variable is an independent Indian literary journal founded and edited by Pravinkumar Ghodake.

The journal explores Indian literature through poetry, ghazals, nazms, shayari, literary prose and other forms of expression, with particular attention to language, cultural context, philosophy and human experience.

Its principal literary foundation encompasses the richness of Urdu, Hindi, Marathi, Kannada, Tamil, Malayalam and Telugu literature, while remaining open to other Indian languages and literary traditions.

The editor's personal intellectual interests extend much further, encompassing Indian and international literature, scientific research, mechanics, philosophy, books, music, cinema, sport and lived experience. These interests inform the perspective brought to the journal, but they do not impose a predetermined direction on its literary selections or reflections.

The present publication scope is focused on Indian literature. International literature and other intellectual disciplines remain part of the editor's wider intellectual life rather than additional compulsory publication categories.

The journal is not intended to function as a conventional academic publication, literary news platform or collection of quotations.

Its identity rests on thoughtful selection, independent interpretation, cultural sensitivity and intellectually responsible writing.

Literature remains at the centre of the publication. Every literary expression deserves to be encountered on its own terms, without an obligation to establish a connection with another discipline.

## 2. Editorial Independence

The publication is independently curated and edited by its founder.

Editorial decisions are guided by literary merit, intellectual relevance, cultural context and the coherence of the growing archive.

Selections are not determined solely by popularity, commercial considerations, social-media trends or prevailing opinion.

The publication retains editorial independence in its selection of authors, literary expressions, languages and themes.

Literary quality, integrity and the potential for meaningful engagement take precedence over the social, cultural, political or popular identity of an author.

Editorial decisions should be based on the merits and suitability of the material rather than external pressure, institutional affiliation, commercial interest or the prominence of the individuals concerned.

The editor retains responsibility for final selection, interpretation, review and publication decisions.

## 3. Literary Selection

Literary expressions may be selected from:

- Classical poetry.
- Contemporary poetry.
- Ghazals and nazms.
- Shayari and couplets.
- Philosophical verses.
- Literary prose.
- Song lyrics and musical poetry.
- Folk and oral literary expressions.
- Regional and linguistic literary traditions.
- Other significant forms of Indian literary expression.

Selections should offer meaningful opportunities for literary, emotional, philosophical or intellectual exploration.

Familiarity and popularity may provide context, but they are not substitutes for literary merit.

The archive should maintain a thoughtful balance between established literary voices, regional traditions, lesser-known expressions and contemporary writing.

Selection should be guided by the character and quality of the individual work rather than a requirement to represent a predefined theme or produce a particular kind of interpretation.

Each selection should provide a meaningful basis for an independent literary exploration.

### 3.1 Weekly Selection Model

The intended publishing format consists of three independent literary explorations brought together within one weekly collection.

The three works may differ in language, literary form, historical period, emotional character and subject.

A shared theme or editorial relationship may be recognised when one genuinely emerges from the selected works, but it is not compulsory.

The weekly format should not force unrelated literary expressions into a single argument or require a predetermined distribution of subjects.

Quality, suitability and verification take precedence over maintaining a fixed publication schedule.

## 4. Language and Cultural Representation

The principal languages of the publication are:

1. Urdu
2. Hindi
3. Marathi
4. Kannada
5. Tamil
6. Malayalam
7. Telugu

Other Indian languages and literary traditions may be considered when they contribute meaningfully to the publication.

Original expressions should be retained in their original scripts wherever possible.

Literary works should be approached with sensitivity to their linguistic structures, cultural contexts, historical backgrounds and distinctive forms of expression.

No language or literary tradition is considered inherently superior to another.

The publication recognises that translation cannot always preserve every nuance of the original expression.

Regional vocabulary, dialectal usage, historical meanings and culturally specific expressions should not be unnecessarily standardised or removed.

The editor's wider literary interests include works from India and across the world. However, the present editorial scope of Verse & Variable is focused on Indian literature. This is a publication focus, not a limitation on the editor's reading or intellectual interests.

## 5. Original Text, Transliteration and Translation

Where appropriate, an individual article should provide:

- The original literary expression.
- The original author's name or qualified attribution.
- The original language.
- Transliteration.
- Pronunciation guidance, where useful.
- An English translation.
- Relevant source information.

Transliteration should preserve the character of the original language as accurately as reasonably possible.

English translations should aim to communicate meaning, tone and literary intention rather than mechanically replace individual words.

Where multiple interpretations are possible, the commentary may acknowledge that ambiguity.

An independent interpretation must not be presented as an authoritative or definitive translation.

Published translations, original editorial translations, collaborative translations and adaptations should be distinguished accurately.

The original expression remains the primary literary reference, and its linguistic and cultural character should not be unnecessarily altered to accommodate the English rendering.

## 6. Attribution and Verification

Attribution is an essential editorial responsibility.

Before publication, reasonable efforts should be made to establish:

- The original author or poet.
- The correct wording of the expression.
- The original language.
- The literary or musical source.
- Relevant publication or recording information.
- The version or edition being discussed, where relevant.

For musical works, the following roles should be distinguished wherever information is available:

- Lyricist.
- Composer.
- Singer or performer.
- Original poet, where applicable.
- Translator or adapter.
- Relevant editor or compiler.

A singer should not be credited as the writer of lyrics merely because they performed the song.

Traditional, collective, anonymous or disputed works should be attributed with appropriate qualifications.

Where attribution remains uncertain, the uncertainty should be disclosed.

The publication does not knowingly present disputed or unverified attribution as established fact.

## 7. Article Structure

The publication follows a weekly collection model.

Each weekly collection contains three independent literary explorations.

Each exploration has its own permanent article page.

An individual article generally contains:

1. Title and publication metadata.
2. Original literary expression, where appropriate and lawful to reproduce.
3. Author and source attribution.
4. Transliteration, where appropriate.
5. Pronunciation guidance, where useful.
6. English translation, where appropriate.
7. Original editorial reflection.
8. Relevant references and acknowledgements, where necessary.

The editorial reflection should contain **no more than four concise English prose paragraphs**. Fewer paragraphs are entirely acceptable when they allow the thought to remain complete and coherent.

There is no mandatory paragraph-by-paragraph formula. The structure, length and direction of a reflection should be determined by the literary expression and the depth of engagement it warrants.

Shorter reflections should not be artificially expanded merely to satisfy a numerical requirement.

The reflection may develop through movements of literary encounter, interpretation, personal perspective and lasting thought. These are possible developments of an idea, not compulsory sections or a prescribed sequence.

No paragraph is reserved for scientific or interdisciplinary discussion.

Ancillary elements such as quotations, translations, headings, illustrations, attribution and source notes are separate from the principal English prose reflection.

The weekly collection page should introduce the three individual articles and provide an organised way to explore them. It should complement rather than replace their permanent pages.

## 8. Literary Interpretation

Literary commentary should engage with the actual content and character of the selected expression.

Depending on the work, this may involve:

- Emotional meaning.
- Philosophical implications.
- Language and imagery.
- Metaphor and symbolism.
- Human relationships.
- Memory and experience.
- Cultural and historical context.
- Ambiguity and multiple interpretations.
- The relationship between form and meaning.
- Personal observations and lived experience.

Interpretations should be grounded in the literary expression rather than imposed upon it.

The writing should follow the movement of the work: its imagery, atmosphere, emotional shifts, contradictions and underlying questions.

The publication does not claim that every literary work has one definitive interpretation.

A reflection should allow the literary expression to retain its individuality. It need not arrive at a moral, motivational message or predetermined intellectual conclusion.

The editor's personal observations and wider intellectual interests may enrich an interpretation when they arise naturally and contribute meaningfully to the reading.

The literary work itself should remain the principal subject.

## 9. The Editor's Scientific Background and Optional Scientific Context

The editor's scientific research background is an important part of the intellectual perspective underlying Verse & Variable.

It is rooted in theoretical, computational and experimental mechanics, including:

- Nonlinear vibrations.
- Nonlinear dynamical systems.
- Nonlinear elastic waves.
- Nonlinear ultrasonics.
- Harmonic generation and scattering.
- Hysteresis and contact nonlinearity.
- Wave mixing and nonlinear interactions.
- Mechanical and phononic metamaterials.
- Nonlinear optics.
- Time-varying physical systems.
- Inverse problems and inverse design.
- Computational mechanics.
- Physical acoustics.

These research interests inform the editor's analytical habits, curiosity and way of approaching unfamiliar questions.

Their presence in the editor's intellectual background does not make them compulsory subjects for the journal.

Scientific knowledge may enter a reflection when a genuine intellectual connection exists and when it meaningfully deepens an idea or question raised by the literary expression.

The scientific discussion may explore physical mechanisms, mathematical structures, theoretical parallels or carefully qualified conceptual comparisons.

However, the following distinctions must be maintained:

- Analogy is not equivalence.
- Metaphor is not a physical mechanism.
- Speculation is not established scientific knowledge.
- Literary interpretation is not experimental evidence.
- Mathematical resemblance does not automatically establish a shared governing principle.

Scientific connections should deepen the discussion rather than distract from the literary expression.

No poem, couplet, song or literary passage is expected to contain a scientific idea, predict a scientific discovery or demonstrate a physical principle.

A literary work does not need a scientific connection to deserve selection, interpretation or publication.

Scientific research is not an independent submission category, and standalone scientific or engineering papers are not part of the journal's normal literary publication format.

## 10. Scientific Accuracy and References

When a reflection contains substantive scientific statements, those statements should be consistent with established knowledge and supported by appropriate references when necessary.

Relevant sources may include:

- Peer-reviewed research papers.
- Academic books.
- Established scientific publications.
- Reputable technical references.
- Original research and experimental studies.
- Appropriate institutional and technical documentation.

When discussing an established physical phenomenon, the article should distinguish between accepted scientific understanding and the author's own interpretation.

References should be sufficiently identifiable to allow readers to locate the original material.

Unverified claims should not be presented as established scientific conclusions.

Theoretical predictions, numerical observations and experimental findings should be distinguished accurately.

Scientific verification is conditional on the presence of substantive scientific claims.

A literary reflection that does not make scientific assertions does not require a scientific reference section, technical verification or specialist scientific review merely to satisfy a publication format.

The same principle applies to other factual and specialist claims: verification should be appropriate to what the article actually discusses.

## 11. Editorial Voice and Writing Style

The editorial voice is reflective, mature, personal and intellectually independent.

The writing should:

- Use clear and thoughtful English prose.
- Prefer meaningful development over short slogans.
- Maintain a first-person reflective perspective where appropriate.
- Preserve literary sensitivity.
- Give attention to emotional continuity and coherence.
- Begin with the literary expression rather than an imposed abstract framework.
- Allow the editor's personal observations and intellectual background to enter naturally.
- Avoid unnecessary scientific jargon.
- Avoid promotional language.
- Avoid exaggerated claims of originality or significance.
- Avoid artificial comparisons and forced analogies.
- Respect uncertainty and interpretive complexity.
- Remain concise without sacrificing meaningful depth.

The publication should not sound like a promotional platform, a textbook or a collection of generic literary summaries.

Its writing should reflect genuine engagement with the selected work.

The objective is not to make every reflection sound alike, but to maintain a recognisable editorial sensibility while allowing each literary expression to determine its own direction.

## 12. Translation and Interpretation Standards

The publication distinguishes among:

- Original literary wording.
- Literal translation.
- Interpretative translation.
- Literary translation.
- Transliteration.
- Pronunciation guidance.
- Editorial commentary.

These categories should not be confused.

Where the English rendering departs from the literal wording to preserve poetic meaning, this should be understood as an interpretative choice rather than a verbatim translation.

The original expression remains the primary literary reference.

Translations should preserve meaning and tone as faithfully as possible while acknowledging that certain linguistic, cultural and poetic nuances may not have exact English equivalents.

An interpretation should not be presented as the original creator's intention without adequate evidence.

Where meaningful ambiguity exists, it may be preserved and discussed rather than forced into a single definitive explanation.

## 13. Copyright and Permissions

The publication respects the rights of original literary, musical, artistic and scholarly creators.

The inclusion of third-party material requires consideration of:

- Applicable copyright law.
- Relevant exceptions and limitations.
- Permission requirements.
- Attribution.
- Source accuracy.
- Existing licences.
- Separate rights in translations, editions, recordings and visual material.

A source citation alone does not establish permission to reproduce protected material.

Where necessary, the publication will seek permission or use an appropriately licensed alternative.

Original editorial commentary should remain distinguishable from third-party material.

Traditional or public-domain material should be assessed carefully, including any separate rights that may exist in particular editions, translations, recordings or adaptations.

The repository's MIT License applies according to its terms and does not automatically establish rights over third-party literary or artistic works.

Confidential permission correspondence and private rights records should not be placed in the public repository.

## 14. Corrections and Updates

The publication recognises that literary attribution, translation, historical interpretation and scientific understanding may require correction or clarification.

Reasonable corrections may be made when errors are identified.

Examples include:

- Incorrect author attribution.
- Incorrect original wording.
- Translation inaccuracies.
- Historical or cultural errors.
- Linguistic inaccuracies.
- Scientific inaccuracies, where applicable.
- Broken references.
- Incorrect publication metadata.
- Technical or accessibility problems.

Significant corrections should be made transparently where appropriate.

Minor typographical and presentation corrections may be made without a prominent notice where they do not materially change the substance of an article.

The existence of a published article does not make it immune to revision.

Substantial interpretative revisions should be distinguishable from factual corrections where that distinction matters to the reader.

Correction, revision, archival and withdrawal procedures are further addressed in the relevant publication policies.

## 15. Editorial Review

Every article is subject to editorial consideration before publication.

Review may include:

- Literary selection and relevance.
- Original-text accuracy.
- Attribution.
- Translation quality.
- Interpretive coherence.
- Emotional continuity and clarity.
- Cultural and historical sensitivity.
- Linguistic accuracy.
- Scientific accuracy, where applicable.
- Reference quality, where applicable.
- Copyright considerations.
- Ethical and privacy considerations, where applicable.
- Accessibility and technical presentation.
- Consistency with the publication's identity.

Specialist consultation may be obtained where the nature of a particular claim or source warrants it.

Such consultation is not a mandatory procedure for every literary exploration.

The publication is independently edited and does not claim a formal external peer-review process or institutional research ethics approval.

Editorial review should be proportionate to the actual content and claims of the article.

## 16. Corrections, Suggestions and Feedback

Readers may suggest literary expressions, identify attribution errors, recommend references, point out translation or contextual problems, or provide constructive criticism.

Suggestions should ideally include reliable source information.

Concerns about copyright, attribution, privacy, cultural representation, research accuracy or other material editorial matters should be considered appropriately.

The editor retains responsibility for final editorial decisions.

Submission of a suggestion does not guarantee publication or acknowledgement.

Good-faith criticism and evidence-based correction requests should be assessed on their substance rather than dismissed because they challenge an existing interpretation or editorial decision.

## 17. Editorial Independence and Limitations

The publication is an independent literary project focused on Indian literature.

Its interpretations represent the considered views expressed in individual articles and should not automatically be treated as definitive scholarly conclusions.

The journal does not present itself as a conventional academic journal or a scientific publication.

Where scientific knowledge is discussed, the reflection is intended for intellectual exploration and does not replace formal scientific publications, experimental validation or peer-reviewed research.

Where historical, linguistic, philosophical or other factual claims are made, they should be supported by appropriate evidence and qualified where necessary.

The publication does not claim formal external peer review, institutional certification or ethics approval merely because it follows internal editorial and research standards.

The editor's wider intellectual interests remain an important part of the perspective informing the publication, but editorial independence and literary merit remain the basis of its selection and interpretation.

## 18. Continuing Development

The archive is intended to evolve over time.

Its literary depth, language coverage and representation of Indian literary traditions may expand as new works and voices are considered.

The journal may also develop new ways of presenting original scripts, translations, literary context, historical material and editorial reflections.

The editor's scientific background and wider intellectual interests will remain part of the perspective informing the publication.

Scientific connections may appear in individual reflections when they arise naturally and offer genuine value, but they are not a separate publication objective, a required content category or a measure of editorial progress.

Consistency of editorial standards remains important even as the publication grows.

The guiding principle is not to maximise the number of articles, but to maintain the literary, intellectual and human value of each contribution.

---

## Closing Principle

Literature should be approached with sensitivity to language, culture, history and human experience.

Every literary expression deserves the freedom to be read on its own terms.

The editor's wider intellectual life, including scientific research, may inform a reflection when it genuinely adds something to the reading. It should never become an obligation imposed upon the literary work.

Verse & Variable is an Indian literary journal shaped by a wider curiosity about the world and the many ways of understanding it.
