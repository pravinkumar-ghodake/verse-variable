A poem can begin with an invocation, an observation of the natural world, or the image of something that has fallen silent. Each beginning opens a different way of looking at life. This week brings together three literary works from three Indian languages, each offering its own world of thought, feeling, and imagination.

We begin with Kuvempu's *O Nanna Chetana*, an invocation to the human spirit to awaken and expand beyond the limits it has come to accept. From Kannada, we move to Subramania Bharati's *Kakkai Kuruvii*, in which the familiar presence of crows and sparrows becomes an occasion to recognise a wider kinship with the living world. One turns inward towards the possibilities of consciousness; the other looks outward with an expansive sense of belonging.

The third work takes us in a different direction. In *Veena Poovu*, Kumaran Asan begins with the image of a fallen flower and reflects on beauty, transience, and the passage of life. Its contemplation is distinct from the other two, yet equally deserving of our attention. Together, these works invite three independent encounters with literature: an awakening of the mind, an intimacy with the natural world, and a reflection on the fragile beauty of existence.
