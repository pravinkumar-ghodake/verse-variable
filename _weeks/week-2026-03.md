Literature often begins at the point where the familiar world is no longer enough. Sometimes it looks towards an unexplored horizon; sometimes it turns towards the difficulties of the present; sometimes it asks what an individual can contribute to a world that is still taking shape. This week's collection brings together three works from Marathi, Hindi, and Telugu, each approaching its subject through a distinct poetic voice.

We begin with Kusumagraj's *Kolambasache Garvgeet*, a poem that draws upon the figure of Columbus and the vastness of the unknown. Its imagery opens a space for thinking about exploration, uncertainty, and the impulse to venture beyond what is already known. We then encounter Dushyant Kumar's *Ho Gayi Hai Peer Parvat-Si Pighalni Chahiye*, where the language of accumulated pain and the demand for change bring us closer to the tensions of human and public life. The movement from one poem to the other is not simply from the outer world to the inner one; each offers its own way of confronting what lies before us.

The third work, Sri Sri's *Jayabheri*, introduces another voice. Its speaker considers the place of an individual within a much larger collective movement, offering a personal contribution to a world that demands participation. These poems need not arrive at the same destination. One looks towards the unknown, another confronts the weight of the present, and the third considers the possibility of contributing to what comes next. Each deserves to be encountered on its own terms.
