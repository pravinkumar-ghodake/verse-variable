# Contributing to Verse & Variable

Thank you for your interest in Verse & Variable.

Verse & Variable is an independent Indian literary journal devoted to poetry, shayari, ghazals, nazms, literary prose and other expressions across India's diverse languages and literary traditions.

Its editorial direction is guided by a commitment to literary integrity, cultural sensitivity, intellectual independence, accurate attribution and thoughtful interpretation.

The editor's wider intellectual interests include international literature, scientific research, mechanics, philosophy, books, music, cinema, sport and lived experience. These interests inform the editorial perspective but do not expand the journal's present literary scope beyond Indian literature.

Scientific knowledge and specialist expertise may contribute to an individual literary exploration when they genuinely deepen its interpretation. They are not independent publication requirements or compulsory elements of a literary contribution.

This document explains the journal's approach to literary suggestions, corrections, editorial feedback and technical contributions. It does not establish a separate submission channel or guarantee publication.

---

## 1. Editorial Direction

The publication is curated and edited by its founder, Pravinkumar Ghodake.

Contributions and suggestions are welcome, but inclusion in the publication is subject to editorial review.

The journal prioritises:

- Literary merit and interpretative depth.
- Meaningful engagement with Indian literary traditions.
- Accurate linguistic and cultural representation.
- Respect for original literary expression.
- Thoughtful philosophical and human reflection.
- Responsible translation and contextual research.
- Proper attribution and respect for intellectual property.
- Intellectual honesty in factual and scientific discussions, where applicable.

Literary quality and the integrity of the individual work take precedence over popularity, institutional affiliation or an obligation to establish a connection with another discipline.

The journal does not require every contribution to include scientific terminology, interdisciplinary comparisons or a predetermined interpretation.

Weekly collections generally bring together three independent literary explorations. The selected works need not share a common theme, subject or interdisciplinary connection.

Each literary work is approached on its own terms, with attention to its language, imagery, context, emotional character and interpretative possibilities.

Individual editorial commentaries are intended to remain concise, generally comprising no more than four English prose paragraphs. This is an editorial limit rather than a fixed four-part formula. The number and movement of paragraphs should follow the needs of the particular work.

## 2. Literary Suggestions

Readers may suggest Indian literary expressions, including:

- Poems and poetic expressions.
- Ghazals and nazms.
- Shayari and couplets.
- Literary passages.
- Song lyrics and musical poetry.
- Classical and contemporary literary works.
- Folk and oral literary expressions.
- Writers and poets from regional literary traditions.
- Other significant forms of Indian literary expression.

Suggestions from Urdu, Hindi, Marathi, Kannada, Tamil, Malayalam and Telugu traditions are particularly welcome.

Other Indian languages and literary traditions may also be considered as the archive develops.

Suggestions should ideally include:

1. The original literary expression or an appropriate reference to it.
2. The original language and script.
3. The author's name, where known.
4. The source of the expression.
5. Relevant publication or recording details.
6. An explanation of why the work deserves exploration.

An international literary work may inform the editor's broader reading and intellectual perspective, but the current editorial selection of Verse & Variable is focused on Indian literature.

A suggestion does not guarantee selection or publication. Editorial selection remains independent and may take into account literary merit, cultural and linguistic context, source reliability, rights considerations and the coherence of the archive.

## 3. Attribution and Source Verification

Accurate attribution is essential.

Contributors should distinguish between:

- Poet or original writer.
- Lyricist.
- Composer.
- Singer or performer.
- Translator.
- Adapter.
- Publisher or recording source.

A performer must not automatically be identified as the author of lyrics they perform.

When attribution is uncertain, the uncertainty should be stated clearly.

Contributors should provide reliable sources wherever possible, particularly when suggesting lesser-known literary works, regional expressions or disputed attributions.

Where multiple versions of a work exist, identify the version being suggested rather than silently combining different texts.

Suggestions should not present unverified online quotations or repeated secondary attributions as established facts.

## 4. Translations and Transliteration

Original literary expressions should be preserved in their original scripts wherever possible.

Translations should communicate the meaning and literary character of the original without presenting an interpretation as an exact equivalent when it is not.

Contributions involving translations should identify:

- Original language.
- Original author.
- Translator, if known.
- Source of the translation.
- Whether the translation is published, adapted or independently prepared.

Transliteration should respect the linguistic characteristics of the original language.

Where pronunciation assistance is provided, it should be distinguished from transliteration and translation.

Contributors should not silently modify an existing published translation and present it as an unchanged work by its original translator.

## 5. Scientific Context and Specialist Input

Scientific and technical expertise may contribute to a literary exploration when it genuinely helps illuminate a question or idea arising from the selected work.

Relevant areas of the editor's research background include:

- Nonlinear vibrations.
- Nonlinear elastic waves.
- Nonlinear ultrasonics.
- Harmonic generation and scattering.
- Hysteresis and contact nonlinearity.
- Wave mixing and nonlinear interactions.
- Mechanical and phononic metamaterials.
- Nonlinear optics.
- Temporal and spatiotemporal modulation.
- Inverse problems and inverse design.
- Computational mechanics.
- Physical acoustics.

Contributors with relevant expertise may help verify or explain a particular scientific concept when it is actually relevant to an article.

Scientific suggestions should not be introduced merely to create an interdisciplinary appearance. A literary metaphor should not be represented as a scientifically established mechanism, nor should a poet be described as having anticipated a modern scientific discovery without adequate evidence.

Standalone scientific research papers, engineering articles or technical studies are not independent literary submission categories within the present scope of Verse & Variable.

Where scientific context is proposed, it should distinguish established findings from theoretical interpretations, mathematical relationships, analogies and speculative ideas.

Where possible, provide:

- Original research papers.
- DOI links.
- Books or scholarly references.
- Mathematical or physical explanations.
- Relevant experimental or computational evidence.
- Important assumptions and limitations.

Scientific terminology should clarify the discussion rather than displace the literary expression. The literary work should remain central.

## 6. Corrections and Editorial Feedback

Corrections are welcome.

Examples include:

- Incorrect literary attribution.
- Errors in original-language wording.
- Translation inaccuracies.
- Incorrect transliteration or pronunciation guidance.
- Historical or cultural inaccuracies.
- Unsupported claims about an author or literary work.
- Scientific terminology errors, where applicable.
- Broken links.
- Technical website issues.
- Accessibility problems.

Please explain the issue and provide a reliable source or supporting evidence wherever possible.

Reasoned disagreement about literary interpretation is not automatically a factual error. Different interpretations may coexist without requiring one to be treated as a correction.

Editorial decisions remain with the publication's editor.

Significant corrections may be reflected in the published article and documented according to the publication's correction policy.

## 7. Copyright and Permissions

Contributors must have the necessary rights or permissions to submit material for publication.

Do not submit:

- Unauthorised reproductions of copyrighted articles.
- Complete copyrighted song lyrics without an appropriate legal basis.
- Unauthorised translations of copyrighted works.
- Images, illustrations or recordings without appropriate usage rights.
- Material copied from other publications without proper attribution and permission where required.
- Research figures, datasets or other third-party material without checking their licensing conditions.

A source citation does not automatically grant permission to reproduce copyrighted material.

Third-party literary and musical works remain subject to their respective rights and applicable copyright laws.

Where a contribution includes original literary material, the applicable publication and reproduction rights should be clarified before acceptance.

Contributors should disclose any restrictions, prior publication arrangements or third-party rights that affect the proposed use.

## 8. Contribution Licence and Intellectual Property

The repository uses the MIT License for material covered by its licence terms.

Code and technical contributions intended to become part of the repository should be submitted with the understanding that the applicable repository licensing terms will govern their use.

The presence of the MIT License does not automatically establish rights over third-party poems, lyrics, translations, photographs, recordings, research figures or other separately protected material.

Before accepting a literary or other third-party contribution, the publication should establish:

- Who owns the submitted material.
- Whether the contributor has the necessary authority to submit it.
- What permissions are granted for publication and display.
- Whether reproduction, translation, adaptation or other reuse is permitted.
- What attribution is required.
- Whether a separate licence or permission statement is necessary.

Contributors must not submit material under a licence they do not have the right to grant.

Where a literary work, translation or other contribution is intended to have rights terms different from the repository's general licence, those terms should be agreed and recorded explicitly.

Submitting a contribution does not transfer ownership of material to the publication unless a separate agreement expressly establishes that arrangement.

The repository's licensing documentation should remain consistent with any separate notices or permissions applying to individual contributions.

## 9. Technical Contributions

Technical contributions may include:

- Website accessibility improvements.
- Responsive design refinements.
- CSS improvements.
- JavaScript corrections.
- Jekyll and Liquid template improvements.
- Search-engine metadata corrections.
- RSS and sitemap improvements.
- Performance and maintainability improvements.
- Documentation updates.
- Multilingual typography and presentation improvements.

Please keep changes focused, understandable and consistent with the existing architecture.

Avoid introducing unnecessary dependencies.

Preserve the multilingual character and visual identity of the publication, including its research-inspired wave design and existing visual language.

Technical contributions are distinct from literary suggestions and editorial contributions. Their acceptance does not imply a change in the journal's editorial scope.

## 10. Pull Request Guidelines

Pull requests are intended for changes to the repository, such as technical improvements, documentation updates and appropriate corrections to existing website content.

A pull request is a repository-change mechanism; it does not, by itself, constitute a formal literary submission or guarantee editorial consideration.

When submitting a pull request:

1. Explain what has been changed.
2. Identify the affected files.
3. Describe why the change is necessary.
4. Include sources for factual, linguistic or attribution corrections.
5. Identify any third-party material introduced.
6. Confirm that you have the necessary rights to submit the contribution.
7. Identify any licence or permission conditions that apply.
8. Explain any technical or compatibility implications where relevant.

Small, focused pull requests are preferred.

Changes should remain consistent with the existing repository structure, documentation and website architecture.

Submissions may be reviewed, modified, declined or deferred at the editor's discretion.

## 11. Respectful Participation

Verse & Variable welcomes thoughtful intellectual disagreement and constructive engagement.

Contributions should remain respectful towards literary traditions, languages, authors, translators, readers and relevant areas of knowledge.

Criticism should address ideas and evidence rather than individuals or cultural identities.

The publication seeks to maintain an environment in which different literary voices, interpretations and intellectual perspectives can be considered with fairness and care.

## 12. Editorial Independence

Submitting a suggestion or contribution does not guarantee publication or authorship credit.

The editor retains responsibility for the selection, interpretation, organisation and final presentation of published material.

The editor may request additional sources, linguistic clarification, translation revisions, specialist feedback, rights documentation or other necessary changes.

The publication aims to maintain its intellectual independence and editorial coherence.

All published contributions should remain consistent with its central purpose as an independent Indian literary journal.

## 13. Closing Thought

A literary expression deserves careful reading.

A translation deserves linguistic responsibility.

A historical claim deserves reliable evidence.

A scientific idea, when relevant, deserves careful reasoning.

The purpose of Verse & Variable is to preserve the integrity of literature while allowing the wider intellectual curiosity of its editor and contributors to enrich its exploration where appropriate.

The literary work should remain free to speak in its own voice.

Thank you for helping preserve that spirit.
