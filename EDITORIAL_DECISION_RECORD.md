## 1. Purpose

This document establishes a structured record for editorial decisions concerning individual articles and other substantial contributions to Verse & Variable.

The purpose is to ensure that publication decisions are:

- Reasoned and consistent.
- Based on identifiable editorial criteria.
- Supported by appropriate source verification.
- Sensitive to linguistic and cultural context.
- Scientifically responsible where technical material is involved.
- Consistent with copyright and ethical requirements.
- Transparent about limitations and unresolved concerns.
- Traceable through appropriate editorial records.

An editorial decision should reflect the actual material examined and the checks completed.

The existence of this record does not establish that an article has undergone formal academic peer review, institutional research ethics approval, or independent scientific certification.

## 2. Scope

This record may be used for:

- Individual literary explorations.
- Poetry and shayari articles.
- Translation and transliteration projects.
- Historical and cultural discussions.
- Philosophical commentary.
- Scientific and interdisciplinary articles.
- Research explanations.
- Interview-based contributions.
- Oral-history material.
- Weekly collection pages.
- Substantial revisions to previously published articles.

A record should be proportionate to the complexity and risk of the material.

A simple literary article does not require the same level of technical assessment as an article making detailed claims about nonlinear mechanics, experimental results, or computational methods.

## 3. Record Classification and Confidentiality

This template is intended to be publicly available as editorial documentation.

Individual completed records should be classified according to their contents.

### 3.1 Suggested Internal Classifications

| Classification | Description |
|---|---|
| Editorial working record | Ordinary internal editorial decisions |
| Restricted editorial record | Unpublished drafts, private reviewer comments, or sensitive correspondence |
| Confidential record | Personal information, consent records, protected sources, or legally sensitive material |
| Public editorial note | A deliberately prepared explanation suitable for publication |

These are internal handling categories, not formal legal classifications.

### 3.2 Public Repository Restriction

Do not commit completed confidential editorial records to the public repository.

In particular, avoid placing the following in public GitHub files, issues, pull requests, or commit messages:

- Private reviewer identities or comments.
- Participant consent forms.
- Unpublished interview transcripts.
- Personal contact information.
- Confidential correspondence.
- Restricted archival material.
- Sensitive allegations.
- Unpublished research data.
- Private editorial deliberations.

The blank template may remain public. Completed records should be stored separately using appropriate access controls.

### 3.3 Public Editorial Explanations

Where readers would benefit from knowing the reason for a substantial editorial decision, prepare a separate public-facing explanation.

Do not disclose confidential internal discussions merely to demonstrate that an editorial process occurred.

## 4. Editorial Decision Principles

Every decision should be guided by the following principles.

### 4.1 Accuracy

Important literary, linguistic, historical, philosophical, and scientific claims should be examined against appropriate sources.

### 4.2 Relevance

The article should have a clear purpose and should contribute meaningfully to the publication.

### 4.3 Proportionality

The level of review and documentation should reflect the complexity, importance, and potential risk of the article.

### 4.4 Independence

Editorial decisions should not be improperly influenced by personal relationships, institutional pressure, commercial interests, or the preferences of an individual reviewer.

### 4.5 Intellectual Honesty

The article should distinguish established knowledge, interpretation, analogy, hypothesis, and speculation.

### 4.6 Cultural and Linguistic Responsibility

Original literary expression, translation, attribution, and cultural context should be treated carefully.

### 4.7 Ethical Responsibility

Privacy, consent, confidentiality, cultural sensitivity, and potential harm should be considered before publication.

### 4.8 Accountability

The editor should be able to explain the principal reasons for a decision based on the available editorial record.

## 6. Editorial Decision Categories

The following definitions provide consistent terminology across article records.

### 6.1 Approved for Publication

The article has undergone the appropriate editorial checks for its scope, and no outstanding issue prevents publication.

This decision does not constitute a guarantee of factual perfection or formal academic validation.

### 6.2 Approved Subject to Minor Revisions

The article is suitable for publication after limited changes that do not require a substantial reassessment.

### 6.3 Substantive Revisions Required

The article requires meaningful changes to its argument, interpretation, sources, translation, or technical presentation.

A further assessment may be necessary after revision.

### 6.4 Additional Assessment Required

A relevant question cannot be resolved adequately through the existing editorial assessment.

Additional specialist advice, independent verification, or further documentation may be appropriate.

### 6.5 Publication on Hold

Publication is temporarily suspended because an important issue remains unresolved.

The reason and conditions for reconsideration should be recorded.

### 6.6 Not Approved for Publication

The article does not meet the publication's requirements, and the identified problems cannot reasonably be resolved within the proposed submission.

### 6.7 Withdrawn

The article is no longer being considered for publication.

The record should distinguish a voluntary withdrawal from an editorial decision not to publish.

## 7. Editorial Decision and Specialist Advice

The final editorial decision belongs to the responsible editor.

Specialist reviewers may provide valuable assessments within their areas of expertise, but their recommendations should not automatically be treated as decisions covering the entire article.

For interdisciplinary work, more than one specialist may be required.

The editor should consider:

- The reviewer's actual expertise.
- The scope of the assessment.
- The evidence supporting the recommendation.
- Any declared conflicts of interest.
- Reasoned disagreement between specialists.
- The limits of the available evidence.

A reviewer should not be presented as endorsing an entire article when they examined only one part.

## 8. Decision Reconsideration

An editorial decision may be reconsidered when significant new information becomes available.

Examples include:

- Discovery of a material source error.
- A previously unavailable original text.
- A correction or retraction of a major research source.
- New evidence concerning attribution.
- A substantiated privacy or consent concern.
- A material translation error.
- A previously unidentified conflict of interest.
- A rights-holder objection.
- A serious technical or scientific error.

Reconsideration should be based on the new information and its actual relevance.

A disagreement with an editorial conclusion does not automatically invalidate the original decision.

### 8.1 Record of Reconsideration

When a decision is revised, preserve an appropriate record of:

- The original decision.
- The new information.
- The reason for reconsideration.
- Additional checks undertaken.
- The revised outcome.
- Any public correction or clarification required.

## 9. Post-Publication Monitoring

Editorial responsibility does not end when an article is published.

Where a significant issue is reported or discovered, evaluate it under the relevant publication policies.

Examples include:

- Incorrect attribution.
- Material translation errors.
- Scientific inaccuracies.
- Inaccurate historical statements.
- Broken or misleading citations.
- Newly discovered rights restrictions.
- Privacy concerns.
- Misrepresentation of reviewer feedback.
- Changes in the status of an important research source.

Possible actions include:

- Correction.
- Clarification.
- Additional disclosure.
- Replacement of an affected resource.
- Temporary restriction.
- Partial withdrawal.
- Full withdrawal.

Refer to `CORRECTIONS_POLICY.md`, `TAKEDOWN_POLICY.md`, and `CONTENT_LIFECYCLE.md`.

## 10. Record Retention and Access

Completed editorial decision records should be retained according to their contents and legitimate editorial purpose.

### 10.1 Appropriate Storage

Use secure private storage for completed records containing confidential or personal information.

### 10.2 Access

Restrict access according to the sensitivity of the material and the needs of the editorial process.

### 10.3 Public and Private Separation

Keep public policy documents separate from internal records.

The public template should not be used as a reason to publish confidential editorial discussions.

### 10.4 Version History

Maintain appropriate version information so that significant decisions can be understood later.

### 10.5 Retention and Disposal

Review retention arrangements periodically.

Delete or securely dispose of material when it is no longer required and no applicable obligation requires its retention.

## 12. Continuing Commitment

An editorial decision should not be an unexplained declaration that an article is ready.

It should reflect a considered assessment of the material, the sources, the methods, the relevant ethical responsibilities, and the limitations of the available evidence.

The purpose of maintaining a decision record is not to create administrative complexity. It is to preserve a clear and proportionate account of why an article was considered suitable, what questions were examined, and what limitations remain.

Verse & Variable seeks to combine literary sensitivity with intellectual independence, scientific responsibility, and transparent editorial judgement.

**A responsible publication decision is one that can be explained, examined, and reconsidered when new evidence warrants it.**
