## 1. Purpose

This policy establishes the principles and procedures for requesting the temporary restriction or removal of material published on Verse & Variable.

The publication maintains a multilingual literary and intellectual archive containing original commentary, literary quotations, translations, historical and cultural material, scientific discussions, illustrations, and other resources.

Although reasonable care is taken in selecting and publishing material, circumstances may arise in which continued public availability of particular content requires review.

This policy provides a framework for handling requests involving:

- Copyright and intellectual property.
- Unauthorised reproduction.
- Personal information and privacy.
- Confidential or sensitive material.
- Incorrect identification of individuals.
- Legal requirements and formal notices.
- Safety and security concerns.
- Materially misleading or seriously problematic content.
- Third-party rights and licensing issues.

The objective is to handle such concerns fairly, proportionately, and transparently while respecting applicable rights, editorial independence, and the integrity of the archive.

## 2. Scope

This policy applies to all publicly accessible material maintained by Verse & Variable, including:

- Individual literary explorations.
- Original literary expressions and quotations.
- Translations and adaptations.
- Historical and cultural commentary.
- Philosophical and scientific articles.
- Research references.
- Images, photographs, and illustrations.
- Audio and video material.
- Publication documentation.
- Publicly accessible repository resources.
- Search indexes, feeds, and machine-readable resources.

The policy also applies when a particular resource is reproduced or linked through multiple pages of the publication.

A request concerning one asset may therefore require review of all locations where that asset appears.

## 3. Fundamental Principles

### 3.1 Responsible Review

Every sufficiently specific and credible removal concern should be considered on its merits.

A request should not be dismissed merely because the affected material has already been published.

### 3.2 Proportionate Action

The response should be proportionate to the nature and seriousness of the concern.

Removing an entire article may be unnecessary when the issue can be resolved by removing one image, replacing a translation, correcting attribution, or restricting a particular passage.

### 3.3 Respect for Rights

The publication should respect applicable copyright, privacy, intellectual property, and other legal rights.

The fact that material is accessible online does not automatically establish permission to reproduce it.

### 3.4 Editorial Independence

A request for removal does not automatically establish that the content is unlawful or factually incorrect.

The publication retains responsibility for its editorial decisions, subject to applicable legal obligations.

### 3.5 Transparency

Material removals should be documented appropriately.

Where suitable, readers should be informed that an article or resource has been withdrawn or revised.

However, public explanations must not expose confidential information or repeat the material that gave rise to the concern.

### 3.6 Confidentiality

Information submitted in connection with a removal request should be handled carefully.

Personal, confidential, legal, or sensitive supporting documents should not be disclosed publicly without an appropriate basis.

## 4. Distinction Between Corrections and Takedowns

A correction and a takedown are different editorial actions.

| Request | Primary objective | Possible outcome |
|---|---|---|
| Correction | Correct inaccurate or incomplete information | Revised text or attribution |
| Clarification | Explain an ambiguity or limitation | Additional explanatory note |
| Rights request | Address a possible intellectual property infringement | Attribution, permission, replacement, or removal |
| Privacy request | Address inappropriate disclosure of personal information | Redaction, restriction, or removal |
| Takedown request | Restrict or remove specified content | Temporary or permanent removal |
| Legal notice | Address a formal legal demand or requirement | Review and appropriate legal or technical action |

A concern may involve more than one category.

For example, an incorrectly attributed translation may require both an editorial correction and a review of the relevant reproduction rights.

Refer to `CORRECTIONS_POLICY.md` for ordinary factual and editorial corrections.

## 5. Grounds for Requesting Removal

### 5.1 Copyright Infringement

A request may identify material that is allegedly reproduced without appropriate permission or another applicable legal basis.

Examples include:

- Copyrighted poems.
- Song lyrics.
- Existing translations.
- Book excerpts.
- Photographs.
- Illustrations.
- Research figures.
- Audio recordings.
- Video material.
- Other protected creative works.

A copyright concern should identify the affected material and explain the basis of the request.

Public availability, attribution, or a link to an original source does not automatically establish permission to reproduce protected material.

### 5.2 Ownership and Licensing Disputes

A request may concern:

- Incorrect licensing information.
- Misrepresented public-domain status.
- Unauthorised adaptation.
- Missing licence conditions.
- Reproduction beyond an applicable licence.
- Material uploaded by someone without the necessary authority.
- Conflicting ownership claims.

Where relevant, the publication may request information establishing the requester's relationship to the rights holder or material.

The existence of a claim alone should not be treated as conclusive proof of ownership.

### 5.3 Privacy and Personal Information

Removal or restriction may be requested where material exposes personal information inappropriately.

Examples include:

- Private contact details.
- Residential addresses.
- Government identification information.
- Private correspondence.
- Confidential documents.
- Unnecessary personal photographs.
- Sensitive information published without an appropriate basis.
- Incorrect or misleading personal identification.

Such concerns may justify precautionary action while the underlying issue is examined.

### 5.4 Confidential Material

A request may identify material that should not have been made publicly available.

Examples include:

- Unpublished confidential manuscripts.
- Restricted institutional documents.
- Private communications.
- Confidential research material.
- Documents shared for a limited purpose.
- Material subject to legitimate confidentiality obligations.

The publication should distinguish genuinely confidential information from material that is merely controversial, critical, or unfavourable to an individual or institution.

### 5.5 Incorrect Identification and Attribution

A removal or restriction request may arise from:

- Incorrect identification of a person.
- False attribution of authorship.
- Use of an individual's identity in a misleading context.
- Publication of a work under the wrong creator's name.
- Unjustified association with an institution or organisation.

Some concerns may be resolved through correction rather than complete removal.

### 5.6 Legal Requirements

Requests may identify a court order, statutory requirement, official legal notice, or another legally relevant instrument requiring review.

Such material should be examined carefully.

A communication describing itself as a legal notice does not automatically establish that it is valid, enforceable, or applicable to the publication.

### 5.7 Safety and Security

Requests may concern material that creates a credible risk to someone's safety or exposes security-sensitive information.

Examples include:

- Exposed credentials.
- Private access information.
- Sensitive personal identifiers.
- Information that materially enables unauthorised access.
- Security vulnerabilities inadvertently disclosed in public documentation.
- Other serious safety concerns.

Security-related reports should also follow `SECURITY.md`.

### 5.8 Other Serious Editorial Concerns

A removal request may also identify material that presents a serious and substantiated ethical or factual problem.

However, disagreement with an interpretation, criticism of a person or institution, or dislike of an editorial position does not automatically justify removal.

Such concerns should ordinarily be examined through the editorial and corrections processes.

## 6. Who May Submit a Request?

Requests may be submitted by:

- Authors and creators.
- Copyright holders.
- Authorised representatives.
- Translators.
- Publishers and rights administrators.
- Individuals whose personal information is affected.
- People whose identity is incorrectly represented.
- Institutions with a relevant legal or rights-based interest.
- Other individuals able to provide credible evidence of a serious concern.

A person submitting a request on behalf of someone else should explain their authority or relationship to the affected material where relevant.

The publication may request reasonable additional information when necessary to assess the request.

## 7. How to Submit a Takedown Request

Takedown requests may be submitted through the editor's official website:

https://sites.google.com/view/pravinkumarghodake

The request should identify the affected material and explain the concern as specifically as reasonably possible.

### 7.1 Recommended Request Information

**1. Requester information**

- Name or appropriate identifying information.
- Relevant role or relationship to the material.
- Preferred means of communication through the submitted channel.

**2. Affected material**

- Exact URL of the relevant page.
- Title of the article or resource.
- Specific passage, image, recording, or other material.
- Location within the page, where relevant.

**3. Nature of the request**

Identify whether the concern involves:

- Copyright.
- Licensing.
- Privacy.
- Confidentiality.
- Personal identification.
- Legal requirements.
- Safety or security.
- Another serious issue.

**4. Explanation**

Explain why the material should be restricted or removed.

**5. Supporting information**

Where relevant, provide:

- Original publication.
- Copyright or ownership information.
- Licence details.
- Permission documentation.
- Original source.
- Relevant legal instrument.
- Evidence of confidentiality.
- Explanation of the privacy or safety concern.

**6. Requested action**

Specify whether the request concerns:

- One passage.
- One image or asset.
- A translation.
- An individual article.
- A weekly collection.
- Multiple related pages.
- A temporary restriction.
- Complete removal.

### 7.2 Accuracy of Submitted Information

Requests should be accurate and made in good faith.

Do not knowingly submit false ownership claims, fabricated legal notices, or misleading information.

### 7.3 Sensitive Supporting Documents

Do not submit passwords, financial credentials, unnecessary government identification details, or unrelated sensitive personal information.

Where a document contains sensitive information that is not necessary for review, redact the irrelevant portions before submission.

## 8. Initial Assessment

On receiving a removal request, the editor should identify the nature and apparent seriousness of the concern.

The initial assessment may consider:

- Whether the affected material can be identified.
- Whether the requester has explained the relevant concern.
- Whether the issue involves an identifiable right or legal obligation.
- Whether supporting evidence has been provided.
- Whether continued public availability may create immediate harm.
- Whether the matter can be resolved without removing the entire article.

Not every request will contain enough information for an immediate decision.

Additional clarification may be requested.

## 9. Temporary Restriction

In certain circumstances, temporarily restricting access may be appropriate while the request is evaluated.

### 9.1 Possible Grounds

Precautionary restriction may be considered where:

- A credible copyright claim requires examination.
- Sensitive personal information has been exposed.
- Confidential material appears to have been published.
- A serious security concern is identified.
- A formal legal instrument requires urgent attention.
- Continued publication may cause significant and immediate harm.

### 9.2 Proportionate Restriction

Where practical, restrict only the affected material.

Possible measures include:

- Removing one image.
- Temporarily disabling an embedded recording.
- Restricting a particular page.
- Removing a sensitive passage.
- Temporarily replacing an affected article with a notice.

### 9.3 No Automatic Admission

Temporary restriction is a precautionary measure.

It does not automatically establish that the requester's allegations are correct or that the publication has admitted liability.

### 9.4 Review After Restriction

A temporary restriction should be reviewed in light of the available evidence.

The publication should determine whether to:

- Restore the original material.
- Modify the affected content.
- Replace the relevant material.
- Retain the restriction.
- Proceed with complete removal.

## 10. Review of Copyright Requests

Copyright requests require particular attention because individual components of an article may have different creators and rights holders.

For example, an article may contain:

- An original literary quotation.
- A separately protected English translation.
- Original commentary.
- A third-party photograph.
- An independently licensed scientific illustration.

The rights position of one component does not automatically determine the rights position of every other component.

### 10.1 Information to Examine

Depending on the circumstances, review:

- Identity of the original creator.
- Identity of the rights holder.
- Source publication.
- Publication date.
- Applicable licence.
- Permission status.
- Extent of reproduction.
- Relevant copyright exceptions or legal basis.
- Whether the request covers all or only part of the material.

### 10.2 Possible Outcomes

A copyright concern may result in:

- Correct attribution.
- Licence clarification.
- Replacement with a properly licensed resource.
- Removal of an image or passage.
- Obtaining appropriate permission.
- Reducing the reproduced material where legally appropriate.
- Removal of the affected article.

### 10.3 Repository Licence Distinction

The repository contains a root `LICENSE` file specifying the MIT License.

That licence applies according to its terms to material within its scope.

It does not automatically provide permission to reproduce third-party literary works, translations, songs, photographs, recordings, research figures, or other independently protected material.

The existence of the MIT License should not be interpreted as a blanket licence covering every asset in the website.

Refer to `COPYRIGHT.md` and `ASSET_CREDITS.md`.

## 11. Review of Privacy and Personal Information Requests

Privacy-related requests should be examined according to the nature of the information and the circumstances of publication.

### 11.1 Information to Examine

Consider:

- What personal information is exposed?
- Does the material identify an individual?
- Is the information genuinely necessary for the article?
- Was the information intentionally made public by the individual?
- Is there a legitimate editorial or scholarly purpose?
- Does continued publication create an avoidable privacy or safety concern?
- Can the issue be resolved through redaction or reduced disclosure?

### 11.2 Possible Outcomes

Actions may include:

- Removing unnecessary personal details.
- Redacting a sensitive document.
- Replacing an image.
- Correcting an incorrect identity.
- Restricting the affected page.
- Removing the entire article where appropriate.

### 11.3 Privacy and Legal Rights

A request concerning personal information should not automatically be interpreted as establishing a particular legal entitlement.

Applicable law, context, legitimate interests, and the nature of the information may all matter.

Where legal rights or obligations require specialised assessment, appropriate legal advice may be necessary.

Refer to `PRIVACY.md`.

## 12. Legal Notices and Formal Orders

A communication that identifies itself as a legal notice or formal demand should be examined carefully.

### 12.1 Documents for Review

Where applicable, the review may consider:

- Identity and authority of the issuing person or body.
- Nature of the legal instrument.
- Relevant jurisdiction.
- Specific material identified.
- Applicable legal provisions.
- Scope of the requested action.
- Relevant dates and deadlines.
- Method of service or communication.
- Whether the instrument is authentic and applicable.

### 12.2 Legal Demands

Do not assume that every demand for removal is legally binding merely because it uses formal language.

Equally, do not disregard a genuine court order or other applicable legal obligation.

### 12.3 Legal Advice

This policy is an editorial and operational framework.

It is not a substitute for legal advice.

Where a request raises a substantial legal question, the appropriate course may include obtaining qualified legal advice or following the relevant platform's formal legal process.

### 12.4 Platform-Level Notices

GitHub and other service providers may operate independent procedures for legal complaints, copyright notices, or other content restrictions.

A report made through the publication's editorial contact route does not replace any formal process required by the hosting provider.

Likewise, a platform-level notice may require separate handling under the relevant provider's policies.

## 13. Review Outcomes

After examination, the publication may reach one of the following outcomes.

| Outcome | Description |
|---|---|
| Request accepted | The evidence supports the requested action |
| Partially accepted | Only specific parts of the request are supported |
| Correction sufficient | The issue can be resolved without removal |
| Replacement | A particular resource is replaced with a suitable alternative |
| Temporary restriction continued | Additional review remains necessary |
| Request declined | Available evidence does not justify the requested action |
| Further information required | The request cannot yet be assessed adequately |
| Article withdrawn | Continued publication is considered inappropriate |
| Referred to platform process | A relevant hosting-provider procedure applies |

The selected outcome should be proportionate to the issue.

## 14. Partial Removal and Content Preservation

Where only part of an article is problematic, the publication should consider whether the remaining material can reasonably remain available.

For example:

- A copyrighted image may be removed while the original commentary remains.
- An existing translation may be replaced with an independently prepared translation, subject to the relevant rights.
- An incorrect personal detail may be redacted.
- A defective citation may be corrected.
- An unauthorised recording may be replaced with a link to an authorised source.

However, partial removal should not be used to retain material where the underlying problem affects the entire article.

The decision should consider both the nature of the concern and the integrity of the remaining content.

## 15. Withdrawal of an Entire Article

Complete withdrawal may be appropriate where a serious concern cannot be resolved through a narrower measure.

Examples include:

- Fundamental uncertainty about the right to publish the central material.
- Unresolvable authorship or source problems.
- Material that should never have been made public.
- Serious factual misrepresentation affecting the entire article.
- A legal requirement to remove the full page.
- Significant ethical or safety concerns.

Withdrawal should be treated as a distinct publication decision, not as an ordinary formatting or editorial revision.

### 15.1 Weekly Collection Review

Where an individual article is withdrawn, inspect the associated weekly collection.

The weekly collection should not continue displaying a broken link or misleading description of the removed work.

### 15.2 Related Pages

Review other places where the affected material appears, including:

- Archive pages.
- Homepage highlights.
- Language indexes.
- Search indexes.
- RSS feed.
- Sitemap.
- Structured metadata.
- Social previews.
- Related-article links.

Where appropriate, update these resources to reflect the removal.

### 15.3 Public Withdrawal Notice

A brief public notice may be appropriate.

Suggested wording:

> This material has been withdrawn following an editorial review. The publication's other content remains available through the archive.

The notice should not reveal confidential details or reproduce the material that was removed.

## 16. Search Engines and Cached Copies

Removing material from the publication does not automatically remove every external copy or search-engine reference.

Previously published content may remain temporarily visible through:

- Search-engine caches or indexed snippets.
- RSS readers.
- External archives.
- Browser caches.
- Social-media previews.
- Third-party websites.
- Independent mirrors.
- Screenshots or downloaded copies.

The publication can update its own pages and request appropriate reprocessing where supported, but it cannot guarantee the removal of copies held or indexed by independent third parties.

### 16.1 Search-Engine Removal Requests

Where appropriate, the editor may consider supported processes offered by search engines for:

- Updated content.
- Outdated snippets.
- Personal information.
- Certain legally restricted material.

Such requests are separate from removing the underlying material from the publication.

### 16.2 Third-Party Copies

A request concerning material copied by an independent third party may need to be directed to that website, platform, or relevant rights holder.

Verse & Variable cannot directly control content published by unrelated services.

## 17. Repository and Hosting Considerations

Verse & Variable is maintained as a static publication using GitHub Pages.

This creates several distinct content locations and technical considerations.

### 17.1 Public Website

The public website displays the generated pages and associated resources.

Removing a page from the active site may require changes to the corresponding source file and any dependent templates or resources.

### 17.2 Git Repository

The source repository maintains the files used to build the publication.

Removing a file from the latest repository version does not automatically erase its earlier Git history.

### 17.3 Git History

Git commits may continue to preserve earlier versions of material.

Where there is a genuine need to remove sensitive information from repository history, additional technical action may be required.

Such action should be considered carefully because rewriting history can affect repository references, existing clones, and collaboration.

### 17.4 GitHub Platform

GitHub may independently restrict access to material or repositories under its applicable policies and procedures.

The publication cannot guarantee that every platform-level decision can be reversed or managed through its own editorial process.

### 17.5 External Backups and Copies

Previously downloaded files, cloned repositories, browser caches, and independent backups may remain outside the publication's control.

Public removal should not be described as guaranteed erasure of every historical copy.

## 18. Public and Internal Records

Takedown requests may require internal documentation to establish what was reported and what action was taken.

An internal record may contain:

- Date of request.
- Affected page or resource.
- Nature of the concern.
- Evidence examined.
- Interim measures.
- Final decision.
- Publication changes.
- Relevant technical actions.

Such records should be limited to information reasonably necessary for the review.

Do not publish private correspondence, sensitive documents, or personal information merely to demonstrate that a removal request was received.

### 18.1 Public Records

Where appropriate, public documentation may identify:

- Affected article.
- General nature of the action.
- Date of withdrawal or restriction.
- Whether the change was editorial or technical.

The extent of disclosure should be appropriate to the circumstances.

## 19. Requests That Are Not Accepted

A request may be declined when:

- The requester has not established a relevant basis for the demand.
- The supporting information is insufficient.
- The alleged material cannot be identified.
- The request concerns a legitimate difference of opinion.
- The material is accurately presented and appropriately sourced.
- The requested removal would introduce a new factual or attribution error.
- The material is not controlled by the publication.
- Another narrower measure adequately addresses the concern.
- The request is inconsistent with applicable rights or obligations.

Where appropriate, the publication may explain the decision and identify what additional evidence would be relevant.

Declining a request does not prevent reconsideration if new and credible evidence becomes available.

## 20. Repeated or Abusive Requests

Repeated requests concerning the same material may be consolidated when they raise substantially identical issues.

However, a new request containing materially different evidence or identifying a new concern should be considered separately where appropriate.

The publication may request clarification where communications are abusive, threatening, misleading, or impossible to assess.

A person's prominence or lack of prominence should not determine whether a credible request is considered.

## 21. Review Expectations and Limitations

Removal requests will be assessed according to:

- Nature of the affected content.
- Evidence supplied.
- Legal and ethical implications.
- Potential harm.
- Technical feasibility.
- Scope of the requested action.
- Complexity of the issue.

The publication does not promise a fixed response time or a particular outcome.

Urgent privacy, safety, security, and legal concerns may require different handling from ordinary editorial requests.

Some matters may require additional research, verification, or specialist legal advice.

Submitting a request does not guarantee:

- Immediate removal.
- Permanent removal.
- A particular correction.
- Acceptance of the requester's interpretation.
- Removal from independent search engines.
- Erasure of previously distributed copies.
- Public disclosure of internal review records.
