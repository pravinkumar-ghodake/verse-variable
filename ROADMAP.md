## 1. Vision

Verse & Variable aims to grow into a distinctive multilingual literary and intellectual archive connecting poetry, shayari, literature, philosophy, human experience, and scientific thought.

Its development will be guided by three complementary objectives:

1. Build a substantial and carefully curated archive of literary expressions across languages and traditions.
2. Develop thoughtful commentary that explores the intellectual, philosophical, cultural, and human dimensions of those expressions.
3. Create meaningful interdisciplinary connections with science, mathematics, mechanics, physics, and other fields of knowledge.

The long-term objective is not simply to accumulate articles. It is to develop an archive in which every published exploration contributes something meaningful to the reader's understanding.

## 2. Development Principles

The roadmap is guided by the following principles:

- **Editorial quality before quantity:** The intellectual and literary quality of an exploration takes priority over publication volume.
- **Respect for original expression:** Original languages, scripts, cultural contexts, and literary forms remain central.
- **Accurate attribution:** Authors, poets, lyricists, composers, performers, translators, and other contributors should be identified carefully.
- **Independent interpretation:** Literary commentary should retain its own voice and should not be presented as the definitive meaning of a work.
- **Scientific integrity:** Scientific ideas should be introduced only when the connection is meaningful and intellectually defensible.
- **Open intellectual exploration:** Connections across disciplines should encourage curiosity without confusing metaphor, hypothesis, theory, and evidence.
- **Accessibility:** Multilingual content and interactive features should be developed with accessibility in mind.
- **Sustainable development:** Technical complexity should serve the publication's purpose rather than become an end in itself.

## 3. Phase I — Foundational Infrastructure

**Objective:** Establish a stable, maintainable, and discoverable publication.

### Priorities

- Complete the foundational Jekyll and GitHub Pages architecture.
- Validate the configuration, layouts, includes, collections, and permalink structure.
- Review the integration of CSS, JavaScript, typography, and visual components.
- Verify responsive presentation across desktop, tablet, and mobile screens.
- Validate metadata, canonical URLs, sitemap, and RSS feed.
- Check internal navigation and external links.
- Review repository licensing and third-party material attribution.
- Validate language metadata and right-to-left presentation.
- Test the custom error page and navigation controls.
- Review accessibility and security-related implementation.

### Intended outcome

A dependable publication infrastructure capable of supporting a growing archive without requiring major structural changes for every new article.

## 4. Phase II — Literary Archive Development

**Objective:** Build the central body of the publication.

### Editorial structure

The intended publishing format consists of three independent literary explorations per week, each with its own permanent article page, accompanied by a weekly collection page.

Each exploration will follow the established editorial framework:

- Original literary expression wherever available and appropriate.
- Relevant language and author attribution.
- Transliteration where useful.
- English translation where appropriate.
- Four substantial paragraphs of original English commentary.
- Relevant references and contextual information.

The four-paragraph structure applies to the principal commentary, not to supplementary quotation, translation, bibliographic information, headings, or editorial notes.

The publication cadence may evolve with the demands of research, verification, and editorial preparation. Quality and accuracy take priority over an artificial publishing deadline.

### Literary directions

The archive will explore material from:

- Urdu poetry, ghazals, nazms, and shayari.
- Hindi poetry and literary expressions.
- Marathi literature and poetry.
- Kannada literary traditions.
- Tamil literary traditions.
- Malayalam literature and poetry.
- Telugu poetry and literary works.
- Other Indian and South Asian literary traditions.
- Relevant international literary expressions where they contribute meaningfully to the archive.

### Intended outcome

A growing collection of independent literary explorations that can be read individually while also forming a coherent, searchable intellectual archive.

## 5. Phase III — Language and Cultural Depth

**Objective:** Develop a genuinely multilingual and culturally attentive publication.

### Priorities

- Expand the representation of regional literary traditions.
- Improve original-script presentation and font fallbacks.
- Strengthen Urdu right-to-left and Nastaliq typography.
- Improve transliteration consistency.
- Develop clearer conventions for translations and adaptations.
- Include historical and cultural context where it adds genuine value.
- Improve the identification of writers, lyricists, composers, performers, and translators.
- Preserve distinctions between regional usage, dialect, register, and literary style.
- Develop language-specific archive browsing where useful.

### Intended outcome

An archive that does not treat regional languages as peripheral additions to an English publication, but recognises them as independent literary traditions worthy of serious attention.

## 6. Phase IV — Interdisciplinary Scientific Exploration

**Objective:** Develop the scientific dimension of the publication without reducing literature to scientific terminology.

Potential areas of exploration include:

### Mechanics and nonlinear dynamics

- Nonlinear vibrations.
- Nonlinear elastic waves.
- Nonlinear ultrasonics.
- Hysteresis and contact nonlinearity.
- Harmonic generation and scattering.
- Wave mixing and nonlinear interactions.

### Metamaterials and wave physics

- Mechanical and phononic metamaterials.
- Nonlinear metamaterials.
- Wave manipulation and scattering.
- Nonlinear cloaking and inverse design.
- Time-dependent and time-modulated physical systems.
- Photonic and phononic time crystals.

### Computational and applied science

- Computational mechanics.
- Finite element modelling.
- Inverse problems and optimisation.
- Machine learning in scientific modelling.
- Structural health monitoring.
- Nondestructive testing and evaluation.
- Physical acoustics and wave propagation.

### Editorial approach

Scientific connections should be developed through careful explanation and appropriate distinctions between:

- Literary metaphor.
- Philosophical interpretation.
- Mathematical analogy.
- Established physical principle.
- Theoretical proposal.
- Numerical observation.
- Experimental evidence.

The presence of a scientific analogy is not a requirement for every article. Some literary works should be explored entirely through their language, culture, philosophy, and human significance.

### Intended outcome

An interdisciplinary archive in which literature and science can illuminate one another without losing their individual intellectual identities.

## 7. Phase V — Scientific Illustrations and Interactive Experiences

**Objective:** Introduce visual and interactive elements when they contribute genuine explanatory value.

Possible developments include:

- Conceptual wave visualisations.
- Illustrations of nonlinear wave behaviour.
- Harmonic generation and frequency interaction diagrams.
- Mechanical metamaterial illustrations.
- Time-modulated system representations.
- Interactive explanations of selected scientific concepts.
- Carefully designed visual comparisons between different physical phenomena.

### Development principles

- Visualisations should have clear explanatory purposes.
- Illustrations should include appropriate captions and text alternatives.
- Scientific diagrams should distinguish conceptual representations from experimentally measured results.
- Interactive components should remain usable through keyboards and assistive technologies.
- Motion should respect reduced-motion preferences.
- Essential information should remain available when animations are disabled.

### Intended outcome

A restrained collection of visual resources that makes complex ideas more approachable without turning the publication into an animation-driven website.

## 8. Phase VI — Search, Discovery, and Knowledge Organisation

**Objective:** Make the growing archive easier to explore.

Potential developments include:

- Improved archive navigation.
- Language-based browsing.
- Literary-form categories.
- Author and contributor indexes.
- Thematic collections.
- Search by literary work, author, language, and subject.
- Cross-references between related explorations.
- Dedicated indexes for recurring scientific concepts.
- Better article metadata and structured information.
- Improved machine-readable descriptions of the publication.

### Intended outcome

A connected archive in which readers can discover relationships between literary works, languages, themes, and scientific ideas rather than encountering every article in isolation.

## 9. Phase VII — Accessibility and Technical Sustainability

**Objective:** Maintain the usability and reliability of the publication as it grows.

### Priorities

- Progressive improvement towards WCAG 2.2 Level AA.
- Keyboard and screen-reader testing.
- Improved multilingual reading support.
- Validation of right-to-left text handling.
- Responsive design maintenance.
- Performance optimisation.
- Reduced unnecessary external dependencies.
- Regular review of broken links and outdated references.
- Validation of feeds, sitemaps, and metadata.
- Documentation of technical changes.
- Preservation of source files and publication history.

### Intended outcome

An archive that remains accessible, maintainable, and technically dependable as its content and functionality expand.

## 10. Phase VIII — Editorial Community and Contributions

**Objective:** Consider responsible ways to involve readers, researchers, writers, translators, and other contributors.

Potential developments may include:

- A formal literary submission process.
- Translation contributions.
- Corrections and attribution requests.
- Scholarly references and contextual suggestions.
- Reader feedback.
- Collaborative thematic collections.
- Contributions from different language communities.

Any future contribution process should follow the publication's editorial policy, contribution guidelines, copyright requirements, and privacy principles.

Contributions should be evaluated on their relevance, accuracy, originality, attribution, and alignment with the publication's editorial standards.

Community participation should not compromise editorial independence or the rights of original creators.

## 11. Long-Term Possibilities

As the archive develops, additional opportunities may be considered:

- Curated collections around particular literary themes.
- Special editions devoted to individual languages or literary traditions.
- Essays connecting literature with mathematics, mechanics, physics, and philosophy.
- Research-oriented thematic publications.
- Carefully documented scientific concept collections.
- Educational resources for interdisciplinary reading.
- Collaborations with literary, academic, and cultural communities.
- Additional formats for preserving and presenting the archive.

These possibilities are exploratory directions rather than commitments to specific publication dates or deliverables.

## 12. What Will Not Be Compromised

The development of Verse & Variable should not come at the expense of its central principles.

The publication will seek to avoid:

- Publishing material merely to meet numerical targets.
- Treating literary works as convenient illustrations of scientific theories.
- Presenting speculative claims as established scientific results.
- Misattributing literary or musical contributions.
- Treating translation as an exact substitute for original expression.
- Reducing multilingual representation to superficial token inclusion.
- Using visual effects that undermine reading and accessibility.
- Making unsupported claims about institutional affiliations, endorsements, or scientific validation.
- Allowing technical expansion to obscure the literary purpose of the archive.

## 13. Review and Evolution

This roadmap is a living document.

Priorities may change as the publication develops, new opportunities emerge, technical limitations become apparent, or editorial experience suggests better approaches.

Progress should be evaluated through the quality, accessibility, intellectual integrity, and long-term usefulness of the archive.

The roadmap will be updated when significant changes in direction or development priorities justify doing so.
