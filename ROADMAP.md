## 1. Vision

Verse & Variable aims to grow into a distinctive, multilingual archive devoted to Indian literature, its languages, cultural traditions, philosophical expressions and many dimensions of human experience.

Its development is guided by three complementary objectives:

1. Build a substantial, carefully curated archive of literary expressions across India's languages and literary traditions.
2. Develop thoughtful, original commentary that explores the literary, emotional, philosophical, cultural and human dimensions of those expressions.
3. Create a sustainable digital publication that preserves original literary expression, supports meaningful reading and makes the archive accessible to a wider readership.

The editor's wider intellectual perspective is shaped by literature, scientific research, mechanics, mathematics, philosophy, books, music, cinema, sport and lived experience. These interests may inform individual reflections where they genuinely contribute to understanding a literary work.

The journal does not require literature to connect with science or any other external discipline. A literary expression deserves careful attention on its own terms.

The long-term objective is not simply to accumulate articles. It is to develop a living literary archive in which every published exploration contributes something meaningful to the reader's understanding.

## 2. Development Principles

The roadmap is guided by the following principles:

- **Literary integrity:** The original literary expression, its artistic qualities and its cultural context remain central.
- **Quality before quantity:** The intellectual and literary quality of an exploration takes priority over publication volume.
- **Respect for original expression:** Original languages, scripts, cultural contexts and literary forms deserve attention in their own right.
- **Accurate attribution:** Authors, poets, lyricists, composers, performers, translators and other contributors should be identified carefully.
- **Responsible interpretation:** Literary commentary should retain its own voice and should not be presented as the definitive meaning of a work.
- **Emotional and intellectual depth:** Reflections should engage meaningfully with the language, imagery, emotional movement and human significance of the selected expression.
- **Intellectual independence:** The editor's perspective may draw upon different fields of knowledge without imposing an external framework upon the literary work.
- **Optional specialist context:** Scientific or other specialist knowledge may contribute when a genuine connection arises and the additional context improves the interpretation.
- **Cultural attentiveness:** Regional languages, literary traditions, dialects and forms should be represented with care and accuracy.
- **Accessibility:** Multilingual content and interactive features should be developed with accessibility in mind.
- **Sustainable development:** Technical complexity should serve the publication's purpose rather than become an end in itself.

## 3. Phase I — Foundational Infrastructure and Maintenance

**Objective:** Maintain a stable, accessible, maintainable and discoverable publication.

### Priorities

- Maintain the established Jekyll and GitHub Pages architecture.
- Review configuration, layouts, includes, collections and permalink conventions as the archive develops.
- Preserve the integration of CSS, JavaScript, typography and visual components.
- Verify responsive presentation across desktop, tablet and mobile screens.
- Maintain descriptive metadata, canonical URLs, sitemap and RSS functionality.
- Review internal navigation and external links.
- Maintain appropriate repository licensing and third-party material attribution.
- Validate language metadata and right-to-left presentation.
- Review the custom error page and navigation controls.
- Improve accessibility and security-related implementation.
- Document significant technical changes and maintainable development practices.

### Intended outcome

A dependable publication infrastructure capable of supporting a growing literary archive without requiring major structural changes for every new article.

## 4. Phase II — Literary Archive Development

**Objective:** Build the central body of the publication.

### Editorial structure

The intended publishing format consists of three independent literary explorations per weekly collection, each with its own permanent article page, accompanied by a weekly collection page.

Each individual exploration should preserve the literary work as its starting point and follow the established editorial framework:

- Original literary expression in its original script wherever available and appropriate.
- Relevant language, author and literary-form attribution.
- Transliteration where useful.
- Pronunciation guidance where appropriate.
- English translation where appropriate.
- A considered, original English reflection.
- Relevant literary, historical, cultural or other contextual references where they add genuine value.

### Editorial length and composition

The principal English commentary should generally comprise no more than four concise prose paragraphs.

The number of paragraphs and the movement of the reflection should follow the needs of the individual literary expression. Shorter reflections are entirely acceptable.

The writing should not follow a compulsory paragraph-by-paragraph formula or reserve a particular paragraph for interdisciplinary discussion.

A reflection may develop through an encounter with the literary expression, its imagery and emotional movement, a personal or philosophical perspective, and a lasting thought. These are possible movements rather than mandatory sections.

### Literary directions

The initial archive focuses on:

- Urdu poetry, ghazals, nazms and shayari.
- Hindi poetry and literary expressions.
- Marathi literature and poetry.
- Kannada literary traditions.
- Tamil literary traditions.
- Malayalam literature and poetry.
- Telugu poetry and literary works.
- Other Indian languages and regional literary traditions as the archive develops.

Classical, modern and contemporary works may all be considered.

The editor's wider interest in international literature remains part of his personal reading and intellectual perspective. It does not expand the journal's present editorial scope beyond Indian literature.

### Editorial priorities

- Select works for their literary merit and interpretative possibilities.
- Avoid selecting material merely to satisfy a numerical target.
- Verify attribution and original wording.
- Respect copyright and reproduction restrictions.
- Preserve linguistic and cultural distinctions.
- Allow each work to develop its own interpretative direction.
- Prioritise editorial preparation and accuracy over an artificial publishing deadline.

### Intended outcome

A growing collection of independent Indian literary explorations that can be read individually while also forming a coherent, searchable and carefully curated archive.

## 5. Phase III — Language and Cultural Depth

**Objective:** Develop a genuinely multilingual and culturally attentive literary publication.

### Priorities

- Expand the representation of Indian regional literary traditions.
- Improve original-script presentation and appropriate font fallbacks.
- Strengthen Urdu right-to-left and Nastaliq typography.
- Improve transliteration consistency.
- Develop clearer conventions for translations and adaptations.
- Include historical and cultural context where it adds genuine interpretative value.
- Improve the identification of writers, lyricists, composers, performers and translators.
- Preserve distinctions between regional usage, dialect, register and literary style.
- Develop language-specific archive browsing where useful.
- Review language-specific typography and reading experiences with appropriate care.

### Intended outcome

An archive that does not treat regional languages as peripheral additions to an English publication, but recognises them as independent literary traditions worthy of serious attention.

## 6. Phase IV — Visual Identity and Research-Inspired Presentation

**Objective:** Preserve and thoughtfully develop the publication's distinctive visual identity.

The visual language of Verse & Variable draws inspiration from the editor's background in theoretical, computational and experimental mechanics, particularly nonlinear waves, vibrations, wave interactions and metamaterial systems.

The existing design concept, **The Wave Manuscript**, and its visual inspiration, **Nonlinear Resonance**, form part of the journal's distinctive presentation.

### Priorities

- Preserve the existing research-inspired wavefield and visual language.
- Maintain the visual coherence of the website across its pages.
- Continue refining original SVG illustrations and scientific-inspired visual assets.
- Consider additional illustrations when they genuinely enhance an article or the reading experience.
- Explore conceptual representations of wave propagation, interference, nonlinear response and structured media where appropriate.
- Maintain consistency between scientific visual inspiration and the actual subject being illustrated.
- Provide meaningful captions and text alternatives.
- Distinguish artistic or conceptual visualisations from numerical or experimental results.
- Avoid unnecessary animation and visual effects that interfere with reading.

### Development principles

- Visual elements should serve a clear design or explanatory purpose.
- Scientific illustrations should remain technically responsible whenever they depict actual physical concepts.
- Conceptual artwork should not be presented as experimental evidence.
- Interactive and animated components should remain usable with keyboard navigation and assistive technologies.
- Motion should respect reduced-motion preferences.
- Essential information should remain available when animations are disabled.

The scientific character of the visual identity does not prescribe the editorial subject of individual entries.

### Intended outcome

A recognisable and carefully maintained visual identity that reflects the editor's intellectual background while allowing the literary archive to retain its own character and direction.

## 7. Phase V — Search, Discovery and Knowledge Organisation

**Objective:** Make the growing literary archive easier to navigate, explore and discover.

### Potential developments

- Improved archive navigation.
- Language-based browsing.
- Literary-form categories.
- Author and contributor indexes.
- Thematic literary collections.
- Search by literary expression, author, language and subject.
- Cross-references between related literary explorations.
- Connections between works from different Indian languages where meaningful.
- Better article metadata and structured information.
- Improved machine-readable descriptions of the publication.
- Clearer links between individual articles and weekly collections.

### Development principles

- Categories and tags should represent the actual content of published entries.
- Literary forms, languages, authors and subjects should remain distinguishable.
- Scientific or technical tags should be used only when an individual entry contains substantive scientific or technical discussion.
- Related works should be connected when there is a genuine literary, historical, cultural, philosophical or thematic relationship.
- Machine-readable descriptions should accurately distinguish the publication from the editor's personal research identity.
- Search and discovery features should improve access without imposing artificial classifications on literary works.

### Intended outcome

A connected Indian literary archive in which readers can discover relationships between works, authors, languages, literary forms and themes rather than encountering every article in isolation.

## 8. Phase VI — Accessibility and Technical Sustainability

**Objective:** Maintain the usability, accessibility and reliability of the publication as it grows.

### Priorities

- Progressive improvement towards WCAG 2.2 Level AA.
- Keyboard and screen-reader testing.
- Improved multilingual reading support.
- Validation of right-to-left text handling.
- Responsive design maintenance.
- Performance optimisation.
- Reduced unnecessary external dependencies.
- Regular review of broken links and outdated references.
- Validation of feeds, sitemaps and metadata.
- Documentation of technical changes.
- Preservation of source files and publication history.
- Review of accessibility in original-language text, translations, illustrations and interactive elements.

### Intended outcome

An archive that remains accessible, maintainable and technically dependable as its content and functionality expand.

## 9. Phase VII — Editorial Community and Contributions

**Objective:** Consider responsible ways to involve readers, writers, poets, translators, linguists, historians and other contributors in the development of the archive.

### Potential developments

- A formal literary contribution and submission process.
- Translation contributions.
- Corrections and attribution requests.
- Literary and historical references.
- Linguistic and cultural clarification.
- Reader feedback.
- Collaborative thematic literary collections.
- Contributions from different Indian language communities.
- Specialist verification where a particular article requires subject-specific expertise.

### Development principles

- Contributions should remain within the journal's literary scope.
- Literary expressions should be the foundation of editorial submissions.
- Scientific or other specialist expertise may support an individual exploration when genuinely relevant.
- Standalone scientific research papers, engineering articles and technical studies are not independent literary submission categories.
- All contributions should follow the publication's editorial policy, contribution guidelines, copyright requirements and privacy principles.
- Editorial independence should remain protected.

Community participation should enrich the archive without compromising the rights of original creators or the integrity of the editorial process.

### Intended outcome

A thoughtful contributor ecosystem that broadens linguistic, literary and cultural representation while preserving the journal's independence and editorial standards.

## 10. Long-Term Possibilities

As the archive develops, additional opportunities may be considered:

- Curated collections around particular literary themes.
- Special editions devoted to individual Indian languages or literary traditions.
- Essays exploring literature in relation to philosophy, history, mathematics, science and other fields where meaningful connections genuinely arise.
- Deeper contextual explorations of selected authors and literary movements.
- Educational resources for multilingual literary reading and interpretation.
- Carefully developed translations and comparative literary studies.
- Collaborations with literary, academic and cultural communities.
- Additional formats for preserving and presenting the archive.
- Greater access to the historical and cultural contexts of selected literary works.

These possibilities are exploratory directions rather than commitments to specific publication dates or deliverables.

They do not establish independent scientific or technical publication categories. The literary work remains the foundation of the journal's editorial purpose.

## 11. What Will Not Be Compromised

The development of Verse & Variable should not come at the expense of its central principles.

The publication will seek to avoid:

- Publishing material merely to meet numerical targets.
- Treating literary works as convenient illustrations of scientific theories.
- Forcing scientific, mathematical or interdisciplinary analogies into literary interpretations.
- Presenting speculative claims as established scientific results.
- Suggesting that a poet anticipated a modern scientific discovery without adequate evidence.
- Misattributing literary or musical contributions.
- Treating translation as an exact substitute for original expression.
- Reducing multilingual representation to superficial token inclusion.
- Using visual effects that undermine reading and accessibility.
- Making unsupported claims about institutional affiliations, endorsements or scientific validation.
- Expanding the publication into unrelated standalone scientific or engineering research.
- Allowing technical expansion to obscure the literary purpose of the archive.

The editor's scientific research background and wider intellectual interests remain important parts of the identity behind the publication. They should enrich its development without becoming compulsory conditions for its literary content.

## 12. Review and Evolution

This roadmap is a living document.

Priorities may change as the publication develops, new opportunities emerge, technical limitations become apparent or editorial experience suggests better approaches.

Progress should be evaluated through:

- The literary quality and depth of the archive.
- The accuracy of attribution and contextual research.
- The quality of original-language presentation and translation.
- The diversity and integrity of Indian literary representation.
- The accessibility and usability of the website.
- The reliability and sustainability of the technical architecture.
- The long-term usefulness of the publication to readers.

The roadmap will be updated when significant changes in direction or development priorities justify doing so.
