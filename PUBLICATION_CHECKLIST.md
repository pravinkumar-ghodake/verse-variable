## 1. Purpose

This document establishes the final pre-publication checklist for articles and related editorial material published by Verse & Variable.

Its purpose is to ensure that material has undergone appropriate checks before becoming publicly accessible.

The checklist covers:

- Editorial suitability.
- Literary accuracy.
- Linguistic accuracy.
- Translation and transliteration.
- Historical and cultural context.
- Philosophical interpretation.
- Scientific and mathematical accuracy.
- Research references.
- Copyright and permissions.
- Research ethics and privacy.
- Authorship and disclosures.
- Accessibility.
- Article metadata.
- Website presentation.
- Final publication approval.

The checklist is intended to reduce preventable errors, omissions, misleading claims, and unresolved publication risks.

It does not guarantee that every error will be identified or that an article has received formal academic peer review, institutional ethics approval, or independent scientific certification.

## 2. Scope

This checklist applies to:

- Individual literary exploration articles.
- Poetry and shayari discussions.
- Translation and transliteration articles.
- Historical and cultural explorations.
- Philosophical commentary.
- Scientific and technical articles.
- Interdisciplinary discussions.
- Interview-based contributions.
- Oral-history material.
- Weekly collection pages.
- Substantial article revisions.
- Other substantial editorial content.

Some sections may not apply to every article.

For example, an article discussing a published ghazal may require extensive literary, linguistic, and translation checks but no experimental methodology review.

A technical article discussing nonlinear wave propagation may require mathematical, scientific, computational, and reference checks.

Mark genuinely inapplicable items as `Not applicable` rather than treating them as completed checks.

## 3. Publication Readiness Principles

An article should be published only after its relevant editorial and technical requirements have been addressed.

### 3.1 Accuracy Before Publication

Important claims should be supported by appropriate sources or clearly identified as interpretations, hypotheses, or speculative observations.

### 3.2 Literary Integrity

Original literary expression must be reproduced and attributed responsibly.

### 3.3 Scientific Integrity

Scientific claims must not exceed the available evidence.

### 3.4 Ethical Responsibility

Consent, privacy, cultural sensitivities, confidentiality, and potential harm must be considered.

### 3.5 Rights Clearance

Copyright and usage permissions must be assessed before material is reproduced or distributed.

### 3.6 Accessibility

Readers should be able to access the article across languages, devices, and appropriate assistive technologies.

### 3.7 Technical Reliability

The published page should render correctly, have valid metadata, and be reachable through the intended website structure.

### 3.8 Editorial Accountability

The editor should be able to explain the principal reasons for approving publication.

## 4. Publication Status

Use one of the following internal statuses.

| Status | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Draft | Article is being prepared |
| Editorial review | Initial content assessment is underway |
| Source verification | Literary or research sources are being checked |
| Specialist consultation | Relevant expert feedback is being obtained |
| Revision required | Changes are necessary |
| Rights pending | Permission or licence questions remain unresolved |
| Ethics review | Ethical concerns are being assessed |
| Final quality check | Publication checklist is being completed |
| Approved | Editorial requirements have been addressed |
| Scheduled | Publication timing has been determined |
| Published | Article is publicly available |
| On hold | An unresolved issue prevents publication |
| Withdrawn | Article is no longer being considered |

These are editorial statuses. They are not automatically implemented by Jekyll or GitHub Pages.

## 5. Article Identification Checklist

### 5.1 Basic Information

- [ ] The final article title is established.
- [ ] The proposed URL or permalink is appropriate.
- [ ] The article category is identified.
- [ ] The primary language is correctly identified.
- [ ] Additional languages are recorded where relevant.
- [ ] The article's subject is clear.
- [ ] The relevant weekly collection is identified, if applicable.
- [ ] The article filename follows the repository naming conventions.
- [ ] The article has a unique and appropriate internal identifier where used.

### 5.2 Contributor Information

- [ ] Contributor names are accurate.
- [ ] Author and translator roles are distinguished.
- [ ] Lyricist, composer, performer, and editor roles are distinguished where relevant.
- [ ] Institutional affiliations are accurate.
- [ ] Public attribution reflects the agreed arrangement.
- [ ] Acknowledgements have been checked.
- [ ] No individual is credited without an appropriate basis.

### 5.3 Editorial Purpose

- [ ] The article has a clear central subject.
- [ ] Its purpose is consistent with the publication's identity.
- [ ] The article offers meaningful interpretation or inquiry.
- [ ] Its scope is appropriate.
- [ ] Its limitations are identified where necessary.
- [ ] The discussion does not rely on exaggerated claims of originality or importance.

## 6. Original Literary Material

Complete this section when the article includes poetry, lyrics, literary excerpts, folk material, or other original expression.

### 6.1 Original Text

- [ ] The original title is accurate, where known.
- [ ] The original author or creator is identified where possible.
- [ ] The original language is correct.
- [ ] The original script is preserved where practical.
- [ ] The text has been checked against an identifiable source.
- [ ] Line and stanza order are correct.
- [ ] Important punctuation is preserved.
- [ ] No words have been silently added or removed.
- [ ] Variant readings have been considered where relevant.
- [ ] Uncertain passages are identified appropriately.

### 6.2 Literary Attribution

- [ ] The poet or author is correctly identified.
- [ ] Lyricist and composer are distinguished where relevant.
- [ ] Performer and original creator are distinguished.
- [ ] The collection or publication is identified where possible.
- [ ] Historical attribution claims are supported.
- [ ] Anonymous or traditional works are not assigned invented authorship.
- [ ] Any uncertainty about authorship is communicated.

### 6.3 Literary Form

- [ ] The literary form is accurately identified.
- [ ] Relevant structural characteristics are understood.
- [ ] The interpretation is consistent with the original text.
- [ ] Literary terminology is used appropriately.
- [ ] The work is not forced into an inaccurate classification.

### 6.4 Literary Integrity

- [ ] The original expression remains central to the article.
- [ ] Its imagery and metaphor are interpreted in context.
- [ ] Emotional and cultural dimensions are considered.
- [ ] Multiple plausible interpretations are acknowledged where relevant.
- [ ] The article avoids unsupported claims about authorial intention.
- [ ] The selected passage is not misleadingly detached from its larger work.

## 7. Translation and Transliteration

Complete this section when translation or transliteration is included.

### 7.1 Transliteration

- [ ] The transliteration method is identified or consistently applied.
- [ ] Original pronunciation is represented reasonably.
- [ ] Important diacritical distinctions are considered.
- [ ] Word boundaries are handled consistently.
- [ ] Regional pronunciations are not unnecessarily standardised.
- [ ] The transliteration has been checked against the original text.

### 7.2 Translation

- [ ] The translator is identified correctly.
- [ ] The translation source is identified where applicable.
- [ ] The translation type is established.
- [ ] The original has been compared with the translation.
- [ ] Grammar and vocabulary are checked.
- [ ] Idiomatic expressions are handled appropriately.
- [ ] Cultural meanings are preserved or explained.
- [ ] Important ambiguity is not silently removed.
- [ ] The translation does not invent meaning.
- [ ] Adaptation is identified where relevant.
- [ ] Published translations are not presented as newly prepared translations.
- [ ] Permission or another appropriate legal basis has been assessed.

### 7.3 Translation Presentation

- [ ] Original and translated lines correspond appropriately.
- [ ] Line structure is readable.
- [ ] The translator's contribution is acknowledged.
- [ ] Translation notes are included where useful.
- [ ] Any material AI-assisted translation is disclosed appropriately.

## 8. Historical and Cultural Verification

Complete this section where historical or cultural claims appear.

- [ ] Relevant dates have been checked.
- [ ] Names and locations have been verified.
- [ ] Historical periods are accurately identified.
- [ ] Literary movements are described appropriately.
- [ ] Primary documents have been examined where practicable.
- [ ] Secondary sources are identified accurately.
- [ ] Conflicting historical accounts are acknowledged where relevant.
- [ ] Oral recollections are distinguished from independently established facts.
- [ ] Regional and community distinctions are preserved.
- [ ] Cultural terminology is used respectfully.
- [ ] Sacred or restricted knowledge has been considered appropriately.
- [ ] The article avoids stereotyping or unsupported generalisations.
- [ ] Historical uncertainty is communicated honestly.

## 9. Philosophical and Interpretative Review

Complete this section where philosophical discussion or conceptual interpretation is involved.

- [ ] Important concepts are defined.
- [ ] Philosophical terminology is used accurately.
- [ ] Thinkers and traditions are correctly attributed.
- [ ] Quotations and paraphrases are accurate.
- [ ] Arguments are distinguished from opinions.
- [ ] Premises and conclusions are not conflated.
- [ ] Competing interpretations are acknowledged where relevant.
- [ ] Comparisons between traditions preserve important differences.
- [ ] Literary interpretation is not presented as proof of authorial philosophical intention without evidence.
- [ ] Speculative conclusions are identified appropriately.

## 10. Scientific and Technical Verification

Complete this section when substantive scientific or technical claims are included.

### 10.1 General Scientific Accuracy

- [ ] Scientific terminology is accurate.
- [ ] Governing principles are correctly described.
- [ ] Established knowledge is distinguished from hypotheses.
- [ ] Theoretical predictions are identified as such.
- [ ] Experimental findings are described accurately.
- [ ] Mathematical analogies are distinguished from physical mechanisms.
- [ ] The article does not overstate scientific certainty.
- [ ] Relevant assumptions and limitations are identified.

### 10.2 Mathematical Formulations

- [ ] Equations are mathematically consistent.
- [ ] Variables are defined.
- [ ] Units are identified where necessary.
- [ ] Dimensional consistency has been examined.
- [ ] Sign conventions are consistent.
- [ ] Boundary and initial conditions are identified where relevant.
- [ ] Equations are presented in a readable format.
- [ ] Important derivations or sources are identified.

### 10.3 Nonlinear Mechanics and Wave Physics

Where applicable:

- [ ] Linear and nonlinear behaviour are distinguished.
- [ ] The relevant constitutive assumptions are stated.
- [ ] Material behaviour is described accurately.
- [ ] Amplitude dependence is considered.
- [ ] Wave propagation conditions are identified.
- [ ] Nonlinear interactions are described correctly.
- [ ] Harmonic generation is distinguished from ordinary linear superposition.
- [ ] Harmonic scattering and generation are not conflated.
- [ ] Hysteresis and contact nonlinearity are appropriately described.
- [ ] Relevant attenuation and dispersion effects are considered.

### 10.4 Metamaterials and Wave Control

Where applicable:

- [ ] The material or structural architecture is described.
- [ ] Relevant resonant or periodic elements are identified.
- [ ] Effective properties are not overstated.
- [ ] Dispersion behaviour is interpreted appropriately.
- [ ] Bandgap claims are supported.
- [ ] Frequency and operating conditions are identified.
- [ ] Wave-control mechanisms are described accurately.
- [ ] Cloaking claims are supported by appropriate analysis.
- [ ] Limitations are acknowledged.

### 10.5 Temporal and Spatiotemporal Systems

Where applicable:

- [ ] Temporal modulation is described accurately.
- [ ] Modulation frequency and amplitude are identified where needed.
- [ ] Relevant spatial dependencies are explained.
- [ ] Frequency conversion is treated appropriately.
- [ ] Harmonic generation and wave mixing are distinguished.
- [ ] Time-crystal terminology is used according to the relevant definition.
- [ ] The article does not equate every periodically modulated system with a time crystal.
- [ ] The relevant physical conditions are stated.

### 10.6 Computational Methods

Where applicable:

- [ ] Governing equations are identified.
- [ ] Geometry and material models are described.
- [ ] Initial and boundary conditions are recorded.
- [ ] Numerical methods are identified.
- [ ] Relevant mesh and discretisation choices are described.
- [ ] Solver and time-integration assumptions are stated where necessary.
- [ ] Convergence or stability checks are discussed where relevant.
- [ ] Simulation results are not presented as experimental measurements.
- [ ] Verification and validation are distinguished.
- [ ] Reproducibility limitations are acknowledged.

### 10.7 Experimental Methods

Where applicable:

- [ ] Experimental methods are described.
- [ ] Relevant equipment and measurement methods are identified.
- [ ] Important parameters are provided.
- [ ] Experimental observations are distinguished from interpretations.
- [ ] Measurement uncertainty is considered.
- [ ] Figures and results accurately represent the data.
- [ ] Any material ethical or safety considerations have been addressed.

## 11. Interdisciplinary Quality

Complete this section when the article connects different fields.

- [ ] The relationship between the disciplines is clear.
- [ ] The connection contributes meaningful insight.
- [ ] The article preserves the intellectual independence of each field.
- [ ] Metaphors are not presented as mechanisms.
- [ ] Analogies are not presented as mathematical proofs.
- [ ] Physical claims have appropriate scientific support.
- [ ] Literary interpretation is not subordinated to unnecessary technical exposition.
- [ ] Scientific terminology is introduced naturally.
- [ ] The discussion is understandable to an intellectually curious reader.
- [ ] The conclusion does not claim more than the connection establishes.

## 12. Research Sources and References

- [ ] Important sources are identifiable.
- [ ] References are genuine.
- [ ] Author names are accurate.
- [ ] Titles are correct.
- [ ] Publication details are complete where available.
- [ ] DOI links are checked where provided.
- [ ] URLs are valid.
- [ ] Access dates are recorded where appropriate.
- [ ] Sources actually support the claims attributed to them.
- [ ] Primary and secondary sources are distinguished.
- [ ] Preprints are not described as peer-reviewed publications.
- [ ] Relevant corrections and retractions have been checked.
- [ ] Unavailable sources are identified honestly.
- [ ] The source register is updated where appropriate.

Do not publish fabricated citations or references generated without verification.

## 13. Originality and Contributor Attribution

- [ ] Original contributions are distinguished from reused material.
- [ ] All contributors have been appropriately identified.
- [ ] Authorship roles are accurate.
- [ ] Translation roles are accurate.
- [ ] Collaborative contributions are acknowledged.
- [ ] Plagiarism concerns have been addressed.
- [ ] Previously published material is disclosed.
- [ ] Substantially similar submissions or prior publications are identified.
- [ ] No contributor is listed without an appropriate basis.
- [ ] No unsupported claim of institutional or professional endorsement appears.

## 14. Copyright and Permissions

- [ ] Third-party material has been identified.
- [ ] Copyright ownership has been considered.
- [ ] Relevant rights holders have been identified where possible.
- [ ] Permission has been obtained where necessary.
- [ ] Licence conditions have been checked.
- [ ] Public-domain claims have been assessed.
- [ ] Published translations have been checked separately.
- [ ] Research figures and illustrations have been assessed separately.
- [ ] Image and recording rights have been examined.
- [ ] Software and dataset licences have been considered.
- [ ] Required attribution is present.
- [ ] Modifications are disclosed where required.
- [ ] Any unresolved rights issue has been resolved before publication.
- [ ] The rights register is updated where appropriate.
- [ ] The asset credits are complete.

A missing rights record should not be interpreted as permission.

## 15. Research Ethics and Privacy

- [ ] Personal information has been reviewed.
- [ ] Sensitive information is necessary for the article.
- [ ] Participant consent has been addressed where applicable.
- [ ] Interview recording permissions are established.
- [ ] Publication permissions are established.
- [ ] Confidentiality restrictions are respected.
- [ ] Vulnerable participants receive appropriate safeguards.
- [ ] Children's information is handled with heightened protection.
- [ ] Historical and archival privacy issues are considered.
- [ ] Community and cultural sensitivities are addressed.
- [ ] Data protection requirements have been assessed.
- [ ] Unnecessary identifying details are removed.
- [ ] Potential harm has been evaluated.
- [ ] Relevant institutional or professional approvals have been obtained where required.
- [ ] Unresolved ethical concerns have been addressed before publication.

## 16. AI-Assistance and Disclosure

- [ ] Material AI assistance has been assessed.
- [ ] Relevant AI assistance is disclosed.
- [ ] AI-generated references have been verified.
- [ ] AI-generated translations have been checked.
- [ ] AI-generated scientific explanations have been checked.
- [ ] AI-generated code has been reviewed where applicable.
- [ ] AI-generated images are not misrepresented as authentic evidence.
- [ ] Confidential and sensitive material has been handled appropriately.
- [ ] No fabricated interviews, permissions, citations, or research findings are present.
- [ ] Human editorial responsibility remains clear.

## 17. Reviewer and Specialist Assessment

Where review has been obtained:

- [ ] Reviewer suitability has been considered.
- [ ] Review scope is documented.
- [ ] Relevant conflicts of interest have been disclosed.
- [ ] Feedback is recorded appropriately.
- [ ] Major concerns have been examined.
- [ ] Minor concerns have been considered.
- [ ] Unresolved disagreements are documented.
- [ ] Reviewer limitations are respected.
- [ ] Acknowledgement permission has been checked.
- [ ] The nature of review is described accurately.

Do not describe informal consultation as formal academic peer review.

Where no specialist review has occurred, do not imply otherwise.

## 18. Editorial Writing Quality

- [ ] The opening is clear and engaging.
- [ ] The main argument is coherent.
- [ ] Literary interpretation is developed thoughtfully.
- [ ] Scientific discussion is appropriately detailed.
- [ ] Important terminology is explained.
- [ ] Paragraphs follow a logical progression.
- [ ] Repetition is reduced.
- [ ] Unsupported generalisations are removed.
- [ ] Promotional statements are avoided.
- [ ] The conclusion is reflective and meaningful.
- [ ] The writing is consistent with the publication's editorial identity.
- [ ] All four main commentary paragraphs are substantial and complete.
- [ ] The four-paragraph requirement is satisfied for standard literary explorations.

## 20. Four-Paragraph Article Structure

For the standard literary exploration format:

- [ ] Paragraph One establishes the human and literary foundation.
- [ ] Paragraph Two develops the interpretation.
- [ ] Paragraph Three examines an appropriate scientific or interdisciplinary connection where meaningful.
- [ ] Paragraph Four provides reflection and synthesis.
- [ ] The four paragraphs are original English commentary.
- [ ] Ancillary quotations and translations are not mistakenly counted as commentary paragraphs.
- [ ] No unnecessary additional commentary paragraphs have been introduced.
- [ ] The article maintains continuity and coherence across all four paragraphs.

If an article uses a different format, the reason should be clear and the applicable format should have editorial approval.

- [ ] Social preview works where tested.
- [ ] Internal navigation reaches the article.

## 28. Revisions to Published Articles

A published article should be reconsidered when significant changes occur.

Examples include:

- New source verification.
- Correction of a literary attribution.
- Translation revision.
- Historical clarification.
- Scientific correction.
- Updated research references.
- New rights information.
- Privacy or consent concerns.
- Accessibility improvement.
- Technical correction.

Record substantial changes using the publication's revision and content-lifecycle procedures.

Do not silently alter a published article in a way that misrepresents its previous content or editorial history.

## 31. Continuing Commitment

Publication is not simply the act of making an article visible.

It is the final stage of a process involving careful selection, interpretation, source verification, rights assessment, ethical consideration, and editorial judgement.

Verse & Variable aims to publish material that is thoughtful without being exaggerated, intellectually ambitious without being careless, and accessible without sacrificing depth.

The purpose of this checklist is to make those commitments operational.

**Every article should be worth publishing, and every publication decision should be made responsibly.**
